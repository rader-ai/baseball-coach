# A manageable game day

Prepare a card rather than a long speech. Verify current local rules for batting order, defensive positions, player minimums, substitutions, innings/time limits, scoring, coach pitching, pitch/attempt limits, tee fallback, base running, and required protective equipment. Do not assume “coach pitch” identifies one ruleset.

Assign approved adults to live play and the waiting players. A coach who is pitching cannot simultaneously manage the bench. Where required roles exceed available approved adults, arrange coverage before play; do not silently drop supervision or invent permission.

Use anonymous player labels in a working example. Plan participation over the whole game and season, subject to local rules and readiness. Track who has had an opportunity; adjust for absences and actual innings played. Avoid concentrating all active roles in the most experienced children. Do not place a child at catcher, pitcher-position defense, or another exposed role without appropriate preparation and required equipment.

Before play: inspect the field, check gear, confirm the stop/weather procedure, review rules with the official/opposing coach, and locate the emergency access point. Keep any necessary private emergency information in the league's authorized system, not in generated public cards.

During play: one adult gives the cue; others avoid competing instructions. “Ready before the pitch” is enough. When the ball is dead, use a short reset rather than a lecture. Keep bats controlled and waiting players in the designated area. Follow the league's rules for where adults may stand.

After play: thank opponents and officials, recognize a specific team behavior, account for equipment, and complete supervised guardian handoff. Log one practice need and one successful adjustment; skip public rankings. Use the rules/safety reference before any child-pitching eligibility answer.
