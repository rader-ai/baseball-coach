# Optional coaching wisdom

Use one useful influence for a particular moment, then give one concrete adult action. Leave this layer out when the ordinary cue already works. Names explain provenance when useful; children do not need a celebrity reference. These are our baseball adaptations, not endorsements, credentials, or evidence that elite success transfers to young children.

## Choose by situation

| Moment | Start with one lens | Useful alternative, not an extra layer |
|---|---|---|
| A child makes a mistake | Ted Lasso: kind reset | Pete Carroll: next action |
| A newcomer feels worried | Dawn Staley: connection | Gordon Bombay: enjoyment |
| Children or adults are in conflict | Pat Summitt: respect and listening | Ted Lasso: curiosity |
| Instructions have become complicated | John Wooden: demonstrate a basic skill | Mr. Miyagi: one understandable component |
| Adults demand perfection or winning | Gordon Bombay: wider purpose | Steve Kerr: joy and compassion |

## Add character without adding confusion

Use warm, original comparisons to familiar things: a book, a small step, a box of crayons. For ages 5–8, use at most one short image, immediately followed by the literal action and, when useful, a demonstration. Adults can handle a richer comparison. Check understanding with “Show me what you'll try”; if the image confuses, drop it and demonstrate plainly. No accent, catchphrase imitation, invented quotation, or branded persona is needed.

Give safety instructions literally first, without jokes. Never use a metaphor to minimize pain, fear, or bullying, demand obedience, shame a child, or replace safe mechanics. Avoid culturally obscure references and ambiguous body instructions. Connection does not require smiling, eye contact, physical contact, or private disclosure. Practice plans still build on basics and finish active play with a fun game; safety always overrides completion.

## Selected lessons and their limits

Sources checked **2026-10-11**. Every phrase below is original wording for this skill, not a quotation from the named person or character.

### Ted Lasso — make room for the next try

**Category:** Fictional inspiration. Apple's [official series announcement](https://www.apple.com/tv-pr/news/2025/03/believe-apples-emmy-award-winning-global-hit-ted-lasso-renewed-for-season-four-with-jason-sudeikis-set-to-return-as-star-and-executive-producer/) describes compassion and belief.

**Adult action:** After an error, offer one easier retry, using the child's response to a brief “What felt hard?” if they want to answer.

**Original phrase:** “That play's a page, not the whole book. Let's turn it. I'll roll this one closer.”

**Limit:** A story theme is not developmental evidence. Do not force cheerfulness or substitute charm for preparation.

### Mr. Miyagi — make the small part understandable

**Category:** Fictional inspiration, based on Sony's [official film synopsis](https://www.sonypictures.com/movies/thekaratekid0); the baseball teaching adaptation is ours.

**Adult action:** Demonstrate one component and explain its purpose before the child tries it.

**Original phrase:** “One building block first: show me your ready stance so you're ready for the roll.”

**Limit:** Do not borrow mysterious chores, blind obedience, combat training, or fictional healing.

### Gordon Bombay — keep a reason to enjoy the game

**Category:** Fictional inspiration, from Disney's [official film page](https://movies.disney.com/the-mighty-ducks).

**Adult action:** Recognize one real instance of helping or learning, independent of the score.

**Original phrase for adults:** “We're planting seeds, not pulling on stems. Let's notice the child who helped a teammate get ready.”

**Limit:** Use the broad enjoyment/teamwork theme, not exclusion, screen tactics, or movie outcomes as proof.

### John Wooden — teach the basic skill clearly

**Category:** Documented official philosophy: [Pyramid of Success](https://www.coachwooden.com/pyramid-of-success), including skill, effort, and enthusiasm.

**Adult action:** Replace a string of corrections with one demonstration and repeated manageable attempts.

**Original phrase for adults:** “Give them one stepping stone, not the whole river. Show the step toward the target, then let them try.”

**Limit:** Do not turn effort into perfection demands or a judgment of character.

### Pat Summitt — make respect practical

**Category:** Official legacy account of philosophy: [Pat's Legacy](https://patsummitt.org/pats-legacy/), including listening, respect, and admitting mistakes.

**Adult action:** Resolve competing adult instructions by listening briefly and agreeing who gives the next single cue.

**Original phrase:** “Two steering wheels make a bumpy ride. Let's agree on one voice for this turn.”

**Limit:** Address danger immediately; ordinary conflict can wait until approved supervision is covered. No shame, demanded eye contact, or unquestioning deference.

### Dawn Staley — connect before increasing the challenge

**Category:** First-person coaching reflection: [Staley's essay](https://www.theplayerstribune.com/articles/dawn-staley-south-carolina-coach) on connection, trust, and learning.

**Adult action:** Offer a hesitant child a choice between observing and one easier attempt.

**Original phrase:** “We can start with a pebble-sized try. Watch one roll, then choose whether you'd like a close one.”

**Limit:** Do not compel participation, disclosures, or playing through fear or pain; the essay does not establish youth-treatment efficacy.

### Steve Kerr — protect joy in practice

**Category:** Published conversation about coaching philosophy: [Stanford Medicine conversation](https://med.stanford.edu/news/all-news/2021/05/warriors-coach-steve-kerr-in-conversation-with-dean-lloyd-minor.html).

**Adult action:** Choose an inclusive cooperative game where a missed attempt keeps the child involved.

**Original phrase:** “This game's a shared puzzle. Everyone gets another piece to try.”

**Limit:** Professional experience is not causal evidence for children. Ending practice with a game is this skill's design choice, not an attributed Kerr rule.

### Pete Carroll — describe the next action

**Category:** Published first-person interview: [Seahawks practice discussion](https://www.seahawks.com/news/head-coach-pete-carroll-provides-an-inside-look-at-how-the-seahawks-pract-183396).

**Adult action:** Replace dwelling on an error with one clear cue for the next safe attempt.

**Original phrase:** “Next turn, one job: look at your target before you throw.”

**Limit:** Reject professional nonstop/full-speed demands for children. Preparation and actionable feedback do not justify fatigue, punishment, or excessive repetitions.

The [AAP readiness guidance](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/sports/Pages/Is-Your-Child-Ready-for-Sports.aspx) supplies separate developmental guidance on adapting basic activities and play to readiness. It does not validate these metaphors, fictional examples, or a guaranteed result. Apply the existing [safety and rules guidance](rules-safety-sources.md) throughout.
