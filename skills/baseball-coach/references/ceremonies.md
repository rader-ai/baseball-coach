# Optional team ceremonies

Offer ceremonies when requested or as a clearly optional short addition. They should never crowd out water, pickup, or needed instruction. Do not presume a religious, patriotic, military, or cultural tradition. If the user requests one, preserve choice and provide a comfortable way to observe or opt out without penalty.

An opening can be a one-minute welcome and optional team word. A closing can be one observation of teamwork and a wave, cheer, or quiet acknowledgment. Avoid forced touching, speeches, chants, or public disclosure.

For end-season recognition, give every child comparable attention and an equally valued keepsake if used. Describe something actually observed, such as returning equipment or encouraging a teammate. Do not invent achievements or use “most improved,” body-based labels, teasing awards, or predictions about future ability. If observations are missing, ask for them or use a shared thank-you statement.

Plan accessible seating/routes, a quiet option, pronunciations, and recognition for absent players. Obtain appropriate permission before any photo; a photo is optional, and publishing requires separate authorization. No gift purchases, invitations, or messages are implied by drafting the ceremony.

Set the clock from attendance. Ten children with 30 seconds each need five minutes, plus transitions. Reserve time and visible supervision for quiet recognition within that block, or agree a later handoff with the caregiver; do not silently add it after the advertised finish. If attendance grows, revise timing openly; don't promise individual speeches that cannot fit. An adult coordinates handoff while another presents when staffing allows. Follow the same safeguarding rules as other team activities.
