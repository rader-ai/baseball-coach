# Run the practice, not just the drills

Use this reference when delivering a complete practice or briefing helpers. These are adjustable operating suggestions, not league staffing rules or an evidence-based ideal duration. Use [practices](practices.md) for activities and [rules/safety](rules-safety-sources.md) for applicable safeguards.

## Before, during, and after player time

Show adult preparation separately from the player program. For example, **12 minutes before player minute 0**: five minutes of setup, four of assistant briefing, three of final checks. Name the adults and jobs. State when supervision transfers from guardians; early arrivals remain with their guardians until the coach accepts the handoff. Do not start accepting children and then leave them to finish setup. If children are already in your care, maintain permitted coverage, simplify the plan, or postpone setup/play.

These minutes are a planning allowance for a simple, familiar setup; allow more for a new site or unfamiliar assistants. If early field access is unavailable, put the remaining inspection, setup, and briefing visibly into the scheduled player-time budget and reduce activities. Do not disguise preparation as a warm-up or leave children without coverage. If safe setup/coverage cannot be achieved, postpone.

The player clock includes arrival instructions, demonstrations, attempts, encouragement, retrieval, group changes, water, final game, and wrap-up. A 45-minute program is not 45 minutes of drills plus those extras. Delivery may need to stop early for safety. Scheduled end time never ends supervision: complete each authorized pickup before packing distant equipment or holding an adult afterbrief.

## Brief approved assistants before players

Do not count a parent or spectator as a supervisor without required league clearance. Share a filled [assistant card](../assets/assistant-card.md) and walk through the actual field:

1. **First minute:** state today's one or two goals; assign each group and its waiting area. Choose one cue-giver per activity.
2. **Second minute:** walk the ball/bat path and retrieval route; point out where the adult and waiting children stay. Demonstrate the stop signal with equipment secured.
3. **Third minute:** show the cue, one likely observable difficulty, and the easier retry. Explain briefly what the cue directs attention toward, without claiming a universal technique.
4. **Fourth minute:** assistant teaches back the setup, cue, and stop/retrieval sequence. Agree who starts transitions, when to request help, and the staffing/weather fallback. Fix any uncertainty before opening the station.

Anyone can call an immediate stop. The head coach coordinates group changes and authorizes restart after adults confirm clear lanes, counted children, and readiness. A helper who has not understood the assignment does not independently run it.

Every adult assigned a station is occupied. Three stations with three adults leave no spare coach for intake, weather monitoring, or care; reduce stations if a support role is needed. At a stopped water break, take a brief visible check of safety, turns, and challenge level without walking away from an active group.

## A short teaching loop children can follow

Budget about 30–45 seconds of each activity for an initial demonstration, adjusting as needed. Show the action from where children can see it safely; say one cue; have a child show the ready position or explain the stop signal. Then offer attempts. Watch an observable action—where the ball went, whether the child looked toward the target—not a label such as “lazy” or “uncoordinated.”

If help is needed, give one correction with a demonstration, offer an easier retry, and check what changed. Pause instead of piling on instructions. Use specific encouragement when true: “You waited for the ready signal.” These are design heuristics; neither fewer comments nor a particular cue type is always better for every child or skill.

## Make movement and interruptions visible

At the end of a block: call stop, secure bats/balls, count children, collect only when the lane is closed, then move groups on the head coach's signal. Reset positions before restarting. Include collection within activity time and group swaps within transition time; do not borrow from water or assume children instantly move.

Check whether intended turns fit: subtract demonstration, rest, reset, and collection from the block before estimating repetitions. Helmet fitting and bat control take time. Treat estimates as planning limits, not quotas; rotate who goes first and record who needs an opportunity next time instead of rushing the final child.

For a late player, the guardian keeps supervision until the next planned intake opportunity. At that pause, stop the relevant activity, accept the handoff, and give the same boundaries/stop demonstration. Budget that instruction inside the existing block; remove repetitions, not supervision. Do not send the child through a live station.

For a late assistant, do not open an unstaffed station. Use a preplanned, permitted whole-group alternative or delay/postpone. An arriving approved helper reads the card before taking a role. At a scheduled stop, keep the group visible with equipment secured and budget a brief teach-back before assigning responsibility. If this takes longer, explicitly shorten the next activity; retain needed water and safe transitions. If coverage or comprehension remains inadequate, keep the simpler plan or end safely.

## Close the loop

After the final fun game, use a brief optional recap, specific appreciation, and an optional cheer, wave, or quiet acknowledgment. Then supervised pickup. Only after all handoffs, take **three adult minutes**: one observed success, one difficulty with the setup or instruction, and one agreed adjustment for next time. A solo coach can make the same three-minute note. Discuss behavior without diagnosing or ranking children; keep sensitive matters in the league's approved private process.

Also allow explicit teardown time for equipment still on the field; do not hide it inside the afterbrief. The samples allow two adult minutes for their small setups, increased if needed, after pickup.
