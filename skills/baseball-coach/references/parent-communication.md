# Parent communication

Write to busy caregivers. Lead with the action and confirmed logistics, then explain the purpose. Leave unknown dates, locations, contacts, and policies as explicit brackets. Use the team's approved adult communication channel; do not contact children privately.

A welcome message covers: enjoyment and participation; confirmed arrival/session details; equipment versus optional purchases; water and clothing; weather updates; attendance reporting; approved volunteering; pickup; and a private route for access or safety concerns. Do not solicit medical details in a group reply.

Useful FAQ answers distinguish team intentions from rules:

- **Does my child need experience?** Beginners are welcome; we'll offer simpler versions and build from readiness.
- **Do we need to buy a bat?** Check the team's shared equipment and league requirements first. Do not recommend an unverified certification or expensive purchase.
- **Will everyone play every position?** We aim for varied opportunities over time, within local participation rules, readiness, and protective-equipment requirements. Do not promise an unsafe assignment or universal rotation rule.
- **Can I help?** Offer specific jobs through the league's screening/training process. A willing spectator is not automatically an approved coach.
- **What if my child is nervous or needs an adjustment?** Invite a private caregiver conversation and offer observation, a simpler task, or a gradual start.
- **How do I raise a concern?** Ask for a calm private conversation away from active play. Safety concerns should be raised immediately through the appropriate adult/league channel.

For a difficult message, describe the event without labeling the child, state the expectation, and propose a concrete next step. Do not speculate about motives, diagnoses, or family circumstances. For weather changes, identify the authoritative decision and next update; never imply a decision has been made when it hasn't.

For a concern about position opportunities, acknowledge the concern, review actual participation, and ask what the child hopes to try. Commit to preparation, a recorded rotation, and private follow-up you control. Plan a new opportunity early in the next attended game when rules, readiness, and equipment permit; do not guarantee an exposed position or explain another child's abilities to the parent. Record missed opportunities when games end early and address them at the next session.

Messages remain drafts until sending is explicitly authorized. A request for an email template does not authorize sending it. Keep rosters, phone numbers, photos, health details, and pickup arrangements out of public examples.
