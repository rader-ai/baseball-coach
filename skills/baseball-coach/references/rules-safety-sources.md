# Rules, safety, and primary sources

## Resolve applicability before answering

Identify organization, sport, division, season, regular season versus tournament, and the local rules document. For age-based eligibility, use the organization's league-age method rather than ordinary birthday age. For rules questions, cite the relevant current primary provision with its scope and access date. If the document is missing, give a provisional planning option and mark the rule unresolved; do not invent a universal rule.

For child pitching, establish applicable rules, verified league age, actual recent pitch totals/dates across teams, rest, and catching/position restrictions. Missing history means **eligibility unverified: cannot clear to pitch**; this is not an automatic ban on ordinary catch. All throwing contributes to load, including warm-ups, lessons, and catcher throws; do not invent a conversion into official pitch counts. Rule limits are ceilings, not workout targets or medical clearance. Stop throwing for pain or fatigue; involve the guardian and appropriate healthcare professional rather than diagnosing, treating, or certifying return. Coach-pitch format does not establish eligibility for child pitching elsewhere.

For a suspected concussion, remove the child immediately, with no same-day return; healthcare-provider clearance is required before return. Follow the emergency plan. Call emergency services for danger signs such as seizures, repeated vomiting, worsening confusion, inability to stay awake, or a worsening persistent headache after an impact. This list is not exhaustive; use CDC's linked guidance. The coach does not diagnose severity.

For heat, use current conditions, league policy, water/shade, and individual circumstances to modify, postpone, or cancel. Water remains available. Weakness or faintness means stop, move to a cool place, and obtain appropriate medical help. Collapse, confusion, or impaired consciousness in heat requires emergency services and immediate cooling under the emergency plan; do not give oral fluids with impaired consciousness. Do not diagnose severity, wait for a temperature reading, or use adult fluid doses for children. A short practice is not automatically safe.

Follow current local lightning and air-quality policy. At thunder/lightning, stop and move to a substantial building or enclosed hard-topped vehicle; dugouts and pavilions are not lightning shelters. Little League guidance uses at least 30 minutes after the last thunder/lightning before return; restart that interval with new thunder/lightning and follow any stricter applicable policy. Plan shelter and supervised movement beforehand.

Check current equipment rules. Little League's Safety Code requires batting helmets in practice and games, catcher protection, and no on-deck bat handling in Tee Ball, Minor, and Major divisions. Consult the full current requirements for the activity, including warming up pitchers; soft balls do not waive them.

Safeguarding requirements are organization-specific. Confirm volunteer clearance before assigning duties, training, adult coverage, restroom procedures, reporting, and pickup. Little League's child-protection policy limits one-on-one contact to observable, interruptible interactions near another adult. A quiet correction is not permission for isolation. These plans never waive staffing requirements; if permitted supervision cannot continue, stop.

When offline, state that current rules/conditions could not be verified. Continue drafting general activities with explicit assumptions, but leave eligibility and safety-dependent decisions unresolved.

Historical material is not current authority: AAP's 2012 baseball policy was retired in June 2020; Pitch Smart's 2023 special return-workload material addressed the 2022 season. Older coaching curricula do not override current rules.
