# Five-year-old tee-ball practice: 32 minutes with one adult

Original sample: six beginners; one approved adult **only if league safeguarding policy permits and whole-group supervision is feasible**. If not, secure approved coverage or postpone. Goal: learn the stop signal, stop a rolling ball, and find first base. No bats are needed for this introductory session.

This is an intentionally abbreviated beginner session, not an optimal duration or official age standard.

Equipment: one soft ball, cones, flat first-base marker, water. Mark a visible waiting semicircle outside one rolling lane and a separate running route. Everyone stays together; only one child and one ball are active. Waiting children point to the target or watch for the ready signal. They do not throw independently.

## Before players — 10 adult minutes outside the 32-minute program

Preparation and the post-pickup two-minute teardown/three-minute review below are planning allowances for this small, familiar setup. Increase adult time if the site, gear, or coverage arrangements need more.

- **T−10 to T−5:** coach inspects the site, lays flat markers/cones, and sets the water/waiting area. All lanes stay in the coach's view.
- **T−5 to T−2:** coach rehearses the demonstration, closed-lane collection, stop signal, and care/restroom interruption plan. Confirm permitted guardian coverage before accepting children; guardians caring for their own child are not automatically team assistants.
- **T−2 to T0:** final conditions/coverage check; be ready for intake. Early children remain with guardians until the coach accepts their handoff. Do not leave accepted children to complete setup.

No assistant is assumed. If an approved assistant later joins, keep the whole-group plan; use the late-assistant briefing below before giving responsibility.

## Player clock

| Time | Whole-group activity | Cue / adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 | Welcome, equipment spot, “freeze” demonstration | “Stop and look.” |
| 4–8 | March, reach, and practice a ready stance in personal spaces | “Feet ready.” Seated/reduced movement available |
| 8–14 | First 30 seconds: show a slow roll/stop. Then take turns; child stops and rolls back. Last 30 seconds: stop, collect any stray ball, reset | “Body behind it.” Roll closer/slower / gently to one side |
| 14–17 | Water and transition; ball secured | Count children before restarting |
| 17–23 | First minute: walk route together. Then one runner at a time through first, separate walk back; final 30 seconds close the lane | “Through the base.” Walk / easy run |
| 23–25 | Water and reset into semicircle | No unsupervised activity |
| 25–29 | Final fun game — Feed the Friendly Monster: show one 30-second example; adult rolls to a ready child, who stops it and rolls a pretend snack back through a wide cone gate to the adult. Take turns; children may offer a quiet monster cheer. Final 30 seconds: ball secured | “Body behind it.” Closer straight roll / gentle side roll. No score or elimination |
| 29–32 | One-minute optional favorite moment, one-minute specific appreciation/optional cheer or wave, final minute begins supervised pickup. Leave distant cones until all handoffs | Passing or quiet acknowledgment is fine |

Total: 32 minutes. Batting can wait for a session with appropriate staffing and setup. If a child needs care or the restroom, stop the whole session, secure the ball, and follow the league's approved guardian/coverage procedure. Do not leave the group or send a child alone. If supervision cannot continue, end with supervised handoff. Current weather policy may require modification or cancellation.

In the final game, waiting children stay in the same visible semicircle outside the lane; no extra balls become active. Show the roll first, use one cue, and notice a real action: “You got ready before the ball came.” If time alone runs short, trim earlier turns and keep a brief game; stop immediately when safety requires.

## Coach's observation and interruption card

Watch whether the child is facing the rolling lane before release. “Body behind it” gives a simple place to move toward; it is not a guarantee of technique. If the roll passes to one side, demonstrate getting behind a slower, straight roll, then retry closer and observe whether the child moves toward it. Offer a side roll only when ready. Stop for pain, distress, wandering, or any loss of coverage; do not diagnose the difficulty.

Late child: guardian keeps supervision until a stopped intake opportunity, normally water at 14–17. Use up to one minute of that block for handoff/boundaries; keep needed water time. If orientation needs more, use 17–18 and shorten running turns, retaining the 23-minute block boundary. An approved late assistant reads the [assistant card](../assets/assistant-card.md); if assigning a role, use 17–19 for a whole-group demonstration/teach-back with equipment secured and children visible, leaving 19–23 for supervised route practice. These replace repetitions; neither extends the schedule. If safe intake/briefing cannot fit, keep the simpler plan or postpone participation with guardian supervision.

After minute 32: continue every required guardian handoff. Only after all pickups, allow **two adult minutes** for remaining cones/equipment and **three minutes** for a self-review: one successful setup choice, one difficulty, one next adjustment. Never leave children to pack or review.
