# 60-minute coach-pitch practice

Original sample: ten players around ages 7–8 with mixed readiness; two approved adults; safe separated field sectors. Goal: watch a gentle coach-delivered pitch and choose a throwing target. This is a practice plan, not game pitch/attempt rules.

Equipment: tee, bat, fitted helmets, suitable soft practice balls, an appropriate sound protective screen for the pitched station, cones/flat bases, water. Confirm local equipment requirements and the screen's suitability for the actual balls/feed. Adult A controls hitting and five waiting/active children; B controls five at fielding. A's empty hitting sector must not reach B's group or any waiting area. No child catches behind the hitter. If separation is inadequate, combine groups and run activities sequentially.

A gives gentle pitches from outside the hitter's full bat reach, using the correctly positioned protective screen and a clear safe ball path. Before play, verify the actual feeder position, screen condition/placement, and field layout against league policy and equipment instructions; no universal distance is assumed. Soft balls do not guarantee adequate protection. If the screen or safe protected setup is unavailable, use the tee with A outside bat reach and likely ball paths; never substitute unprotected hardball front feeding. Only one hitter has a bat. Also offer the tee whenever requested or when pitches frustrate the task. Bat parked and all play stopped before retrieval; no live fielders in that sector. B rolls to one child at a time; the child fields and throws toward a broad, empty target. Everyone else waits clear; collection occurs only after “freeze.”

## Before players — 15 adult minutes outside the 60-minute program

Preparation and the post-pickup two-minute teardown/three-minute review below are planning allowances for a simple, familiar setup. Increase adult time when the screen, gear, site, or assistant briefing needs more; do not rush protective setup to fit the allowance.

- **T−15 to T−8:** head coach A inspects the hitting sector, marks safe waiting/walking routes, and verifies the screen/protected feeder setup or chooses tee-only; approved assistant B checks equipment, fielding target, water, and helmet fit options.
- **T−8 to T−3:** A/B walk both stations. Rehearse one demonstration, one observable difficulty and easier retry, stop/retrieval, group assignments, and care/weather fallback. Each adult teaches back their assignment. A coordinates transitions/restarts; either can stop. No competing cues to the same child.
- **T−3 to T0:** verify coverage and readiness for intake. Early arrivals remain with guardians until accepted handoff. More complex setup needs more adult preparation time, not unattended children.

## Player clock

| Time | Activity and adults | Cue / adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Both: check-in, boundaries, demonstrate stop/retrieval | “Freeze and look.” |
| 5–10 | A leads movement and ready stance; B supports | “Ready before the ball.” Walk / add direction change |
| 10–13 | A gives a 45-second hitting demo; B gives a 45-second field/target demo; remaining 90 seconds assign groups and check safe positions | Gear stays parked during movement |
| 13–24 | A: gentle coach pitch or tee, group 1. B: roll, field, target throw, group 2. Include attempts, brief feedback, helmet changes, and stopped collection; final minute secures/counts | Hit: “Watch the ball.” Tee / broad direction goal. Field: “Look, then throw.” Name one target / choose between two |
| 24–28 | Water and supervised swap | Water available throughout |
| 28–39 | Same adults/stations, groups switch. First 45 seconds re-show positions/cue; all reset/collection inside block; final minute secures/counts | Brief turns; no compulsory swing quota |
| 39–43 | Water; secure bats and clear throwing lanes | Both inspect running route |
| 43–53 | First minute: demonstrate route/signals. Then whole-group baserunning, no ball/bat: A starts one runner, B signals stop at first or continue on clear route. Final minute closes lane | “Find the coach.” Walk and pre-name destination / react to signal |
| 53–57 | Final fun game — Mystery Play: equipment secured; A mimes fielding or running while children copy the ready pose in personal spaces and point to the named base. B leads a team celebration after each reveal; children may suggest the next mime | Demonstrate first / add a second destination. Seated poses welcome; everyone stays in |
| 57–60 | One-minute optional reflection, one-minute specific appreciation/optional cheer or wave, final minute begins supervised handoff | Quiet participation is welcome |

Total: 60 minutes. Readiness, not age, controls distance and complexity. Give one cue and specific feedback: “You found your target before throwing.” For a routine time cut, shorten earlier activity blocks while preserving water, transitions, a brief Mystery Play game, and positive wrap-up. Apply current weather policy; stop immediately if conditions or staffing become unsafe, even before the game. No child-pitching eligibility is asserted by this plan.

## Adult teaching notes and fallback

- **A / hitting:** watch whether the child is ready and looking toward the ball. “Watch the ball” gives one thing to attend to. If repeated pitched attempts are confusing, stop, demonstrate contact from a comfortable tee setup, and offer that simpler retry; check the next attempt. Do not add mechanical corrections all at once. Every bat/helmet change and retrieval uses station time; do not promise a fixed number of hits.
- **B / field and choose:** watch whether the child looks at the target before release and whether the ball travels into the designated lane. If the target is missed, demonstrate looking first, offer one closer/wider target, retry, and observe direction. It may clarify the task; it does not diagnose the child. Add a second target only when ready. Stop your group and ask A for unclear rules, care, equipment, or safety concerns while retaining supervision.

Late player: guardian retains care until a stopped intake, normally 24–28. Budget up to one minute of that pause for handoff and boundaries; if more is needed, use 28–29, reducing attempts rather than water or protection.

If B is absent, no stations open. Only with permitted, feasible solo coverage and a confirmed care/restroom plan, A keeps everyone together: 13–24 becomes one-at-a-time rolls returned to A with no child throws; 28–39 becomes bat-free target-pointing and ready-position play. All other block boundaries and breaks remain. If ten children cannot be safely managed this way, delay/postpone; an uncleared parent is not a substitute. A late approved B reads the card and joins a stopped whole-group demo/teach-back at 28–30, leaving 30–39 for the alternative game. Keep the group combined afterward. If intake and briefing need longer, replace more attempts within that block, never water or supervision.

In the solo fallback, A supplies all demonstrations and final-game prompts. At 43–53, A gives start/destination signals from a position that keeps the runner, waiting group, and return route visible. If this cannot work, use whole-group ready poses and destination-pointing instead, in the same block. No second-adult signaling or independent throwing is assumed.

After minute 60: complete every authorized pickup under continued supervision. Then allow **two adult minutes** for remaining gear and **three minutes** for A/B's afterbrief: one observed success, one task/setup difficulty, one adjustment. No adult leaves children to hold the review.
