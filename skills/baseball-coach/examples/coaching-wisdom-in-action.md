# Coaching wisdom in action

Original fictional situations and original coach wording. Each uses one influence from the [optional reference](../references/coaching-wisdom.md); the child-facing response needs no name or attribution.

## A child misses a rolling ball

**Request:** “A six-year-old missed twice and says they're terrible.”

**Usable response:** Offer one closer, slower roll. “That play's a page, not the whole book. Let's turn it. I'll roll this one closer; get your body behind it.” Show the ready position, then let the child try when ready. If the book image doesn't land, simply demonstrate again. Notice the real action afterward: “You moved behind that ball.”

**Basis:** Ted Lasso's compassion as fictional inspiration; the easier retry is our coaching adaptation. Keep other children out of the rolling lane. Pain or distress calls for stopping and appropriate support, not another motivational line.

## A hesitant newcomer watches from the group

**Request:** “Our new five-year-old doesn't want to join the drill.”

**Usable response:** Offer a small choice without requiring an answer about feelings. “We can start with a pebble-sized try. You can watch one roll, then choose whether you'd like a close one.” Demonstrate the short roll to a ready participant while the newcomer stays with the supervised group. Accept watching as the choice; don't negotiate away their pause.

**Basis:** Dawn Staley's first-person reflections on connection and trust; the choice and baseball activity are our adaptation. No claims about the child's psychology or a promised confidence improvement.

## Two adults give competing corrections

**Request:** “My assistant and I keep interrupting each other, and the kids look confused.”

**Usable response:** Agree on one cue-giver for the next turn. At a break, with group supervision maintained, say privately: “Two steering wheels make a bumpy ride. I added to the confusion too. For this round, please give the one cue while I manage the waiting group.” Use a respectful tone, then follow the agreement.

**Basis:** Pat Summitt's documented respect/listening philosophy; wording and role assignment are ours. If the disagreement concerns an immediate hazard, stop play and address it literally first. Do not leave children unattended to resolve the argument.
