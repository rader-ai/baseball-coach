Here is a **35-minute practice for eight five-year-olds and one approved coach**. Teach two basics—stop a rolling ball and run through a finish—then end active play with **Baseball Statues**, a game everyone plays together.

Before accepting the children, confirm the league-approved restroom arrangement. Your permission for solo group supervision is established; restroom coverage is not. A workable option, if the league permits it, is for each child's own guardian to remain available on site for restroom care. Confirm that with all guardians. If it cannot be arranged, obtain approved coverage or postpone.

Assume a clear, level space away from other games and traffic, suitable weather under current league policy, and drinking water beside the visible waiting area. Check the field, emergency access, and local equipment requirements before starting.

Use **one soft ball and four cones**. Secure the three spare balls, tee, and bat at your equipment spot out of children's access. No batting is planned because protective batting gear has not been established. You do not have to use everything available.

Mark a waiting line with two cones, beside and outside the ball lane. The other two mark a short start-to-finish route, about 15 feet long, with room beyond to slow down. Children pass beside the finish cone, never onto it. Show a separate walk-back route that does not cross the active lane. Stand where you can see the active child, waiting children, and water together.

Your stop signal is **“Freeze—hold the ball and look at me.”** All movement stops. Nobody chases a loose ball; stop play before retrieval. If fetching it would take your attention away from the group, take the whole group with you. Only one ball and one child are active during the ball and running activities; the final equipment-free game involves everyone in personal spaces.

| Clock | What to do | One cue and adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 | Count all eight, show the waiting area and water, demonstrate the stop signal, and walk the running and return routes together. | “Stop and look.” Rehearse again if needed. |
| 4–8 | Lead marching, reaching, and a ready stance in personal spaces. Face all eight. Demonstrate briefly, then let them copy. | “Bend your knees.” Easier: seated or standing hand-position practice. |
| 8–16 | Show roll-and-stop. Call one child into the lane, roll gently from a few feet away, and have them stop the ball and roll it back. Give everyone a turn before repeating. Others watch from the waiting line and notice when the ball stops. | “Get behind the ball.” Easier: roll closer and slower. If comfortable, change only the direction with a slight side roll. |
| 16–19 | Park the ball. Take water together, count children, and prepare the running route. | Water is available throughout; stop activity if helping a child would divide your attention. |
| 19–26 | Demonstrate going through the finish, slowing down, and walking back. One child goes at a time; release the next only after the previous child returns. Give everyone a turn before repeats. | “Run past the cone.” Easier: walk the route. A child may watch and point to the finish instead. |
| 26–28 | Water, recount, secure all equipment, and spread into personal spaces for the final game. | Check that everyone has room without touching. |
| 28–32 | **Final fun game: Baseball Statues.** You lead all eight together: call “ready,” and everyone makes the ready pose; call “point to the finish,” and everyone points; call “freeze,” and everyone becomes a statue. Show each action before using it. After a few rounds, invite a child to suggest a silly, safe statue while you keep leading. Nobody is out for moving. | Give only the current action, one at a time. Easier: seated poses, or copy only “ready.” No balls, bats, running, or contact. |
| 32–35 | Gather and count, ask one optional question—“What did you enjoy?”—thank the group, and begin supervised guardian handoffs. | Mention a real action: “You waited until your teammate was back before starting.” Continue supervision for anyone awaiting pickup after minute 35. |

The clock totals **35 minutes**, including demonstrations, transitions, water, the final game, and closing. You supervise the entire group throughout. No independent station or extra throwing group is added.

Keep feedback short and specific: “You got behind that ball.” After a miss: “Let's try it closer.” Offer a pause or simpler turn without labeling the child. If waiting becomes difficult, reduce repeated turns and move to an equipment-free whole-group activity.

**If someone needs the restroom:** stop immediately, secure the ball and bat, gather and count, and summon that child's on-site guardian to you. Complete the league-approved handoff while retaining the other children. Resume only after recounting and settling the group. The guardian returns the child directly to you. Do not leave seven children to escort one, send a child alone, or use a teammate as an escort. If coverage fails, stop the activities and use the league's approved coverage/pickup procedure while continuing supervision. Do not make the child wait for the water break.

For an ordinary time delay, trim repeated rolls or runs and retain a brief Baseball Statues game and positive goodbye. Weather, fatigue, care needs, or loss of workable supervision override that plan: stop when needed, even before the game. Use current league weather policy; shortening the session does not override cancellation.
