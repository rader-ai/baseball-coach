# Frozen scenario prompts

All characters, constraints, quotations, rosters, and policies below are synthetic. These prompts were chosen for ordinary coaching problems, after reading the skill entry point and general references, before producing scenario answers. They were not copied from the repository's evaluation suite. No repository evaluation outputs were read. Each prompt implicitly invokes the frozen Baseball Coach skill.

## S1 — First-time parent, limited attention

I've agreed to coach tee-ball and feel out of my depth. Tomorrow I have seven children aged five and six, 35 minutes, and one approved assistant who has never coached either. The league has confirmed our two-adult setup and guardian handoff process. We've got six soft balls, seven gloves, four cones, one tee, one bat and two fitting helmets that meet our league's requirements. We have an open grass outfield, and may get in 10 minutes early. Guardians stay at the edge of the field. Please give me something I can print and use without learning a lot of baseball vocabulary. I don't need to cover everything on day one.

## S2 — Live disruption, late helper and lost space

It's minute 18 of a 50-minute practice for eleven seven-year-olds. I am coaching and an approved assistant is managing the other group. We'd planned to swap the groups now: six were doing tee hitting and five were rolling and catching. A third approved helper who knows the children has just arrived; she hasn't seen the plan. The groundskeeper says we must leave the outfield now. We can use a fenced grass strip 12 yards wide by 20 yards long beside the parking lot; the gate opens toward the cars. We have soft balls, cones, one tee, one bat and proper helmets. No walls or nets. Two players are frightened of catching; several are asking when they get to hit. What should I do for the remaining time? The league's emergency and pickup arrangements are already established, and guardians are nearby.

## S3 — Mixed experience and a nervous older newcomer

Plan 55 minutes for nine players aged eight through ten in our neighborhood introductory baseball program. Three have played two seasons and want a challenge, four are new, and two can throw but usually turn away from a catch. One of the newcomers is ten and is embarrassed about joining younger children. I have two approved assistants, nine gloves, twelve soft practice balls, cones, four flat bases, one tee, one bat and correctly fitted required protective gear. There is room for two separated activity areas, and we can set up 15 minutes early. The league has approved our adult coverage and care arrangements. I'd like them to work on getting a ground ball to first and making contact, without the experienced players spending the whole hour waiting.
