# 55 minutes: field to first and tee contact

Use three mixed groups of three, with neutral names A, B and C. Each group visits hitting once; the other six players work together on fielding. Offer the same easy and harder choices to everyone, without announcing who is experienced. The ten-year-old newcomer can start with a straight roll or the tee just as anyone else can.

Two activity areas are enough. You run hitting; Assistant 1 feeds ground balls; Assistant 2 receives at a marked “first” and supports the fielding group. There is **one active fielding ball**, not a separate catching station. Both assistants belong to that one activity. All nine are always assigned to an adult.

## Fifteen minutes before intake

Use 6 minutes to inspect and set up, 5 for adult briefing/teach-back, and 4 for final checks and handoffs. Early children stay with guardians until accepted. Confirm current conditions, the league emergency plan and the already approved care arrangements. Mark an empty hitting sector that cannot send errant balls into fielding, and put waiting/water places outside every path. The fielding area needs a receiving end with clear space beyond; do not place it toward hitters or spectators. Use flat bases as markers; no live runners or sliding today. If safe separation is unavailable, run sequential whole-group work and reduce hitting turns.

Set out one tee and bat with required fitted protective gear, six soft balls for hitting, one active soft ball for fielding and the rest secured. Gloves are optional for a gentle roll if the player finds that easier. Adults control all equipment and group changes. Show **FREEZE: stop, hold balls, park the bat, look at the coach**. No child picks up a bat until called; no child catches behind a hitter.

Brief assistants using the cards below; each demonstrates the cue, waiting positions, stopped retrieval and when to ask for help. Tell adults privately: “Everyone gets an entry point and a next challenge. We don't describe a task as being for little kids.” Ask the newcomer quietly in an observable setting whether they'd like to watch one turn or start with a roll. Either choice keeps their invitation to play.

## Player program

| Minutes | Work and groups |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Welcome, count, show FREEZE and boundaries; then comfortable walking and side steps in personal spaces. You show; assistants watch spacing. Children may walk or use seated movement. |
| 5–16 | A hits with you; B and C field with both assistants. Within each 11 minutes: 1 minute demo, about 8 minutes attempts/feedback, 2 minutes stopped collection and preparing to move. |
| 16–19 | All play stops. Secure equipment, water, count and walk A to fielding and B to hitting. Adults stay assigned to the receiving group until transfer is acknowledged. |
| 19–30 | B hits; A and C field. Same 11-minute structure. Fielders start with the familiar action before a challenge. |
| 30–33 | Secure equipment, water and supervised group change. |
| 33–44 | C hits; A and B field. Same structure. Rotate first-turn order so one child is not always last. |
| 44–47 | Collect with play stopped, secure bat, water, count and gather in the fielding area for one shared game. |
| 47–53 | **Final game: Team Delivery.** Details below. |
| 53–55 | Ask for an optional demonstration or answer: “What did you do before the throw?” Notice a real action, offer a cheer or wave, then begin supervised pickup. |

These blocks total 55 minutes. Water is also available between turns, with the relevant activity paused if coverage requires. Pain or fatigue stops throwing. Stop for distress, unsafe equipment use, wandering, weather or loss of coverage. Use the existing care/emergency process; no adult silently leaves their assignment.

## Hitting card — you, three children

**Purpose:** Comfortable contact into the empty sector. One called hitter wears fitted required protection at the tee. You stand outside the entire swing; the two waiting children remain in your sight well away from bat and ball paths. Bats stay with you; there are no practice swings in the waiting area.

**Demo and cue:** Show a controlled swing into the sector: “Send it into that space.” Point at a broad area; it gives the swing a simple purpose. Park the bat before helping with tee position or approaching the hitter.

**Turns:** Start with two swings each before repeats. At roughly 45–60 seconds for fitting/checking, two swings and changing, three first turns need about three minutes. Use the rest for another turn and an individual retry, allowing for stopped collection. All swings stop and the bat is secured before supervised collection; count and reset before restart. No quota justifies rushing the last child.

**Adjust:** First check tee height/location and manageable bat. If contact is difficult, show the same action and make one comfortable tee adjustment. If ready, let the player pick one of two broad directions within the same safe sector, without asking for more power. Watch contact and whether the swing stays controlled. Say, “You sent that toward the place you picked,” only when true. A miss alone does not diagnose mechanics. Experienced children can make purposeful choices without live pitches or a faster swing.

## Fielding card — both assistants, six children

**Places:** Assistant 1 is the feeder in front of the active fielder; Assistant 2 is the first-base target off to the throwing side, with a clear receiving area and space beyond. Mark waiting places outside both the incoming roll and outgoing throw paths. Walk the geometry together before starting. Adjust the starting fielding distance for comfortable throws; do not require the full shortstop distance.

**Sequence:** Assistant 1 calls one child and rolls. The child controls the ball, finds Assistant 2's ready glove, and makes a comfortable throw. Assistant 2 secures the ball. With the fielder and waiting children stationary and no feed active, return that same ball to Assistant 1 through the clear return path. Then change child and confirm readiness. No second ball enters the sequence. A missed ball means everyone holds position while an adult directs stopped collection.

**Cue/demo:** “Stop it, then find first.” Show a slow roll, comfortable control, a look at the target and a gentle throw. The pause separates the catch from the choice. Assistant 1 is the cue-giver; Assistant 2 reports what arrived rather than adding another correction.

**Easier:** Offer a straight slow roll, then roll it to Assistant 2 rather than throw. The two children turning away from catches need not receive airborne throws; nobody throws at them unexpectedly. A child may stop-and-hold while the adult demonstrates the next step. Keep the same invitation to try again.

**Harder:** For a willing player who controls the basic task, use a gentle side roll while keeping the same distance and target. Or on a later turn keep the roll straight and move the target modestly farther. Change one thing at a time. No hard bad-hop blocking, racing a runner, or maximum-effort throws.

**Watch/retry:** Is the ball controlled before the throw begins? Did the child find the ready target? If the throw is rushed, repeat the demo and separate stop from send. Check your own feed before correcting. Use “You checked first was ready” when observed.

**Turns:** One roll-field-send-return-change cycle may take 30–40 seconds; six first turns therefore need roughly 3–4 minutes. With eight attempt minutes, plan about two turns per child per block, fewer if demonstration or retrieval takes longer. Rotate starting order and give an easier retry within the child's next turn. Waiting children can point toward the target on a stopped demonstration; do not invent an unsupervised throwing game to keep them busy.

**Stop/transition:** Anyone can call FREEZE. Assistants keep the entire fielding group visible and call you rather than walking away for a problem. At a change, close the lane, secure/retrieve, count, and move only after your signal. If a care need occupies an assistant, stop and reassess coverage before restart.

## Final game: Team Delivery

All nine join the fielding area; bat and spare balls remain secured. You supervise the waiting children, Assistant 1 rolls to one ready player, Assistant 2 receives at first. Same one-ball route and cue. Each child chooses “straight delivery” or “side delivery,” with rolling onward as the easier choice. The group celebrates each safe sequence; there are no outs, speed targets or public scores. The goal is to complete a team round, not beat another child.

Use 45 seconds to show rules, about 4½ minutes for turns, and 45 seconds to secure the ball and count. With nine children that is only about 30 seconds per turn; if it runs slowly, calmly finish what fits and record who starts next time. Do not hurry throws. A child can point to first or choose the next delivery while taking a pause, and is invited back without pressure.

## Changes and follow-through

- **Assistant late:** Until all required coverage is present, keep guardians responsible if necessary. If your approved arrangements allow the two adults already there to supervise one group, use 0–5 together and 5–16 for ball-free ready movements and a slow whole-group rolling demo. At 16–19, one adult supervises the stopped group while you give the approved newcomer a visible briefing/teach-back; extend by reducing 19–30 if needed. Resume only with understood roles. Do not open an unstaffed station.
- **Late player:** Accept at 16–19 or 30–33 while all equipment is secured. Explain boundaries and FREEZE, reducing the next attempt block if necessary. The guardian remains responsible until accepted.
- **Routine ten-minute cut:** After the 30–33 break, C gets 33–39 hitting with a short turn each while the others field; 39–41 secure/water; 41–44 a brief ball-free Delivery Poses game (everyone points to first, shows stop, then mimes a gentle throw); 44–45 recap. Record reduced turns. Unsafe weather means stop/cancel under policy, not this shortened schedule.

Supervise every required handoff beyond minute 55. Then allow three adult minutes for notes and three for teardown. Record a setup issue, one useful adjustment and missed opportunities without ability rankings. Next time, offer the same easy roll before a reminder to see what children remember; do not treat a good throw today as proof of lasting learning.
