# Your first 35-minute practice

Today needs only two goals: stop a rolling ball and try hitting from the tee. You say the cue; children try; you make it easier if needed. You do not need baseball speeches.

**Say this first:** “When anyone says FREEZE, stop, put your ball down, leave the bat on the ground, and look at me. Only the person I call picks up the bat.” Show it once and let everyone practice.

## Before children join you — 10 adult minutes

- **10–6 minutes before:** You check grass, holes, nearby activity and an empty hitting direction. Your assistant checks equipment and marks the waiting/water area. Confirm current conditions and league weather instructions; this plan assumes conditions permit play. Confirm your emergency procedure and guardian/restroom arrangement before accepting children.
- **6–2 before:** Walk the two activity areas together using the cards below. Each adult shows where children wait, demonstrates their cue, and explains how to stop and collect. Do not run two areas unless even a missed hit or roll cannot enter the other area. If the space does not allow that, use the whole-group option below.
- **2–0 before:** Secure spare equipment and accept handoffs. Until then, children remain with guardians. If checks take longer, keep guardians responsible and use the delayed-start option; don't rush the checks.

**Equipment actually used:** tee, bat, properly fitting required helmets, gloves, six soft balls, four cones. Put two cones around a clearly explained batting waiting area well away from the bat and ball paths, and two around the rolling group's waiting area. Point out boundaries that cones alone cannot show. Water stays with each group, off the activity path. You keep the bat; only one bat is ever out.

You lead hitting with four children; your assistant leads rolling with three. Call them the two groups, without ability labels. After the break they switch. Keep both groups within talking distance, with safely separate ball paths. Every child belongs to one adult throughout.

## The player clock

| Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–4 | Both adults welcome children, count them, show waiting areas and practice FREEZE. Tell them today's two actions. |
| 4–7 | Balls and bat stay parked. In separate personal spaces, everyone follows “walk in place,” “reach,” and “freeze.” Show first; walking or seated movement is welcome. Adults watch their assigned children. |
| 7–15 | First activities. Each adult uses 45 seconds to demonstrate, about six minutes for short turns and easier retries, and the last 75 seconds for stopped collection and getting ready to move. |
| 15–18 | FREEZE, secure equipment, water, count, then walk groups with their adult to the other waiting area. You coordinate the change; no child swaps alone. |
| 18–26 | Second activities, with the same eight-minute budget. Start with a short demonstration for the new group. |
| 26–28 | Secure bat and all balls, water, count, and spread out in personal spaces. |
| 28–33 | **Final game: Ready, Roll, Celebrate.** No equipment is active. You call “ready” and everyone makes their ready pose; call “roll” and children mime putting their glove in a rolling ball's path; call “celebrate” and they choose a small wave or funny safe pose. Show each first, vary the order, and invite an optional child call. Assistant watches spacing. No one is out. Easier: copy the assistant or point while seated. |
| 33–35 | Ask, “What helped you stop the ball?” Passing is fine. Notice one real action, offer a wave, and begin the league's guardian handoff process. |

Water is available whenever needed; pause a station to provide it safely. All instruction, helmet changes, collection and movement are inside these times. If a child needs care, both activities stop while you use the established guardian/coverage process. Do not leave an active group to help elsewhere.

## Your hitting card — take to the tee

**Places:** One called hitter at the tee; others in the marked waiting area where you see them and the hitter. No child stands behind the batter or in the hitting area. Keep yourself, including hands, outside the full swing. Park the bat before approaching the tee. Use three balls for hitting; keep the others secured at their activity/equipment spot.

**Show:** With children outside bat reach, demonstrate one controlled swing. Say, “Send the ball into that empty space.” This gives the child a simple destination.

**Turns:** Check helmet and setup before handing over the bat. Offer two swings, then park the bat and change hitter. Use a stopped collection whenever balls run out: no one swings; bring your whole group into the empty area together, collect, return, count, and reset. The assistant's group remains in its separate space. Plan roughly 45 seconds per child's two-swing turn plus collection; this is an allowance, not a quota. Start with one turn each before offering extras.

**Watch and adjust:** Does the bat reach the ball while the child stays in their space? First check tee position and manageable bat. If needed, park the bat, make one comfortable tee adjustment, demonstrate again, and invite another try. If ready, let the child choose a broad direction within the same empty sector. “You kept your swing in your space” is useful when true. A child may watch or pause without losing their next invitation.

## Assistant's rolling card — hand this over

**Purpose/places:** One child faces you in a clear short rolling lane; the other two or three watch from the waiting area to its side. You see all of them. One ball is active. Other balls remain secured.

**Show and say:** Roll slowly, showing glove near the ground: “Move behind the ball.” You are giving them a place to move toward, not a list of body positions.

**Try:** Call one ready child, roll gently, let them stop it, then roll it back to you. Two rolls per turn, then change. The child can simply point to the path or try a nearer roll. When comfortable, offer one gentle side roll instead of making it faster. Watch whether they moved behind the next roll. Notice that action when it happens.

**Miss/stop:** If it passes, everyone stops while you direct collection; never chase across another activity. Stop for discomfort, fatigue, distress, wandering, unsafe behavior or a care need. Keep your group with you and call the head coach; do not walk away. At a change: ball secured, count, then move only on the head coach's signal. Be ready to show back the places, cue and stop sequence before intake.

## If today changes

- **Only one usable area:** Keep both adults with all seven children. Use the same clock; 7–15 is rolling together, 18–26 is hitting together. The assistant manages waiting children and helmet changes outside the bat area. Offer one short turn each first; do not add another activity to fill the queue.
- **Helper late:** Your confirmed arrangement is two adults. Keep children with guardians until the helper is present and briefed. For a five-minute delay, run a 30-minute player program: 0–4 welcome, 4–6 movement, 6–12 first activities, 12–15 water/swap, 15–21 second activities, 21–23 secure/water, 23–28 game, 28–30 closing. For a longer delay, simplify further or postpone; do not assume solo approval.
- **Late child:** Guardian waits until the 15–18 break. You explain FREEZE and places during that break while the assistant supervises the stopped group. If it takes longer, take the time from 18–26, not the safe handoff.
- **Conditions worsen:** Pause for the current league decision. If conditions allow a shorter session, stop activities at minute 26 and use 26–28 to secure/water, 28–31 for the game, 31–33 for closing. Unsafe conditions mean stopping, not finishing the game.

Keep supervising until every authorized handoff is complete. Then allow two adult minutes to collect remaining equipment and three to ask: What worked? Where did children wait too long? What will we simplify next time? At the next practice, try a familiar slow roll again before explaining; that helps plan the next step, without claiming today's success proves learning.
