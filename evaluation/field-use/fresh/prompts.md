# Fresh field-use scenarios

Written before the revised skill snapshot was frozen. All people and settings are fictional. Evaluators receive these requests and the skill, without baseline answers or reviewer criteria.

## A1 — A first practice I can carry

I am a parent volunteer and tomorrow is my first time leading tee-ball. Eight children are five or six, with mixed attention and no team practice yet. We have 38 minutes, two approved adults including me, and 10 minutes beforehand. The league has confirmed our coverage, emergency plan, and guardian/restroom handoff process. We have eight gloves, eight soft balls, six cones, a tee, one bat and correctly fitted required helmets. I can inspect two separated grass areas before we start; guardians stay at our agreed pickup boundary. I want two simple things to teach and something short I can carry and glance at. My assistant will want her own instructions. Please make the practice, not just tell me how to plan one.

## A2 — The assistant has to leave

We're at minute 21 of a 48-minute practice for nine six-year-olds. My approved assistant has to leave now. We have stopped the tee-hitting and rolling groups, with bat and balls secured. Our league permits me to lead one visible group here, and a guardian can take responsibility for their own child through the established handoff if a care need comes up; I cannot leave the group to fetch anyone. Our grass area and weather remain suitable. Four children haven't hit yet, and two are uneasy about catching. Please tell me what to do next and give me the remaining clock in a short field card. I don't want a long new preparation guide while I'm here.

## B1 — Mixed experience with limited station space

Please plan 50 minutes for ten players aged seven through ten. Four have played before, four are complete beginners, and two can throw but turn away from catches. One older beginner is self-conscious about being in the group. Three approved adults can arrive 12 minutes early. We have ten gloves, twelve soft balls, cones, four flat bases, one tee, one bat and correctly fitted required protective gear. Our approved care and emergency arrangements are in place. There are two separated activity areas: one hitting sector and a rectangular fielding area. The fielding area might fit two adult-led lanes, but we'll have to inspect the buffers and ball paths on arrival. I want them to control a ground ball and send it toward first, plus make comfortable tee contact, and I want to avoid long waits. Give me the card I carry and brief helper cards. If two fielding lanes won't fit, I still need a realistic plan for the same end time.
