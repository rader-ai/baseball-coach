# Carry card — 50 minutes, ten players

**Goals:** Control a slow grounder, then send it toward marked first; make comfortable tee contact. Mix experience into five neutral pairs, A–E; never announce ability groups. Everyone may start close. Three approved adults: **H** leads/hits, **F** fields, **G** runs the second field lane—or supervised ball-free practice in the fallback.

**Places/equipment:** H has tee, bat, four soft balls and fitted required batting protection. Each field lane has four balls, gloves and a flat base marking first beside its adult. Adult rolls toward one child; child controls and sends back toward first. All paths stay inside that lane. Waiting players stand on cones behind and to the side of the active child, outside errant-ball paths. At hitting, the other child waits outside full bat reach and ball paths; H stays outside full swing reach. No child catcher. Only the called batter holds the bat.

**Stop:** Anyone calls “STOP—hold balls, park bat, stay put.” Retrieve only after all activity in that sector stops; no throws/swings during crossing. H restarts after counts and adult readiness. Water is always available. Pain/fatigue stops throwing; distress, care needs, lost coverage or unsafe conditions stop affected play. Use approved care/emergency and current weather policies; stop all stations if an adult must leave a role.

**One-lane fallback:** Keep this clock and hitting pairs. F runs the only ball lane. During each six-minute work period, F takes the listed F quartet for three minutes while G leads the other quartet in safe, ball-free ready/stop/point movements. Swap quartets, stopped, at the midpoint; swap time comes from those blocks. This needs inspected waiting/personal spaces, not a second ball lane. Expect fewer ball attempts: a short introduction to control-and-send. If even these spaces cannot be kept clear, do not open simultaneous stations; stop and reorganize or end safely.

## Detachable card — H, tee and head coach

**Rounds:** 8–43; one pair at a time, as listed. Keep both children visible. Check required protection, manageable bat, footing, tee height and waiting boundary before handing over the bat. No on-deck swings.

**Show/cue:** “Send the ball into that space.” Demonstrate toward the empty sector. Two comfortable swings, then park the bat; everyone stops before supervised collection. Include all retrieval and changeover in a roughly 75-second visit. Alternate hitters; aim for two visits each, without rushing.

**Watch → retry:** If the bat misses, check tee position and comfort; adjust the tee and offer the same easy attempt. When willing and controlled, offer one broad target choice. Praise an actual action: “You kept your swing in your space.”

Coordinate stopped round changes and counts. Anyone entering the sector, unsafe bat control, distress or care needs means stop. If visits run longer, preserve one complete visit for each hitter before extra turns; tell F/G at the next stopped change.

## Detachable card — F, grounder to first

**Rounds:** 8–43; four players at a time. Use your assigned quartet in the two-lane plan; in the fallback take both quartets in the listed order, three minutes each. You stand at the target end beside marked first, facing the active child. Waiters remain behind/to the side of that child in inspected safe places. One ball live.

**Show/cue:** “Stop it, then find your target.” Roll slowly only to a ready child. They secure it, look toward you, then send toward first on your ready signal. Begin with a short roll back or underhand send; comfortable overhand throws are optional. This straight route rehearses control and direction, not a full game decision.

Allow about 25 seconds for ready, roll, control, send, safe recovery and child change. Stop before chasing misses. Give everyone a turn before repeats. A poor feed gets an easier retry, not a mechanical lecture. Ready players may try one gentle side roll. Stop for pain, fatigue, turning away or distress; offer a slower roll or pause, never force a catch.

## Detachable card — G, lane two or fallback movement

**Two safe lanes:** Run your listed quartet with F's grounder routine, cue and stop rules, in your own independent lane. You own that group; you are not a spare supervisor for H or F.

**One lane:** Secure your four spare balls. Keep your quartet in inspected personal spaces outside F's ball paths. With no balls or bats, demonstrate ready position, an imaginary ground-ball stop, and pointing toward first. “Stop it, then find your target.” Let everyone move together without racing; walk or point as an easier option. These movements do not count as ball attempts.

At the work period's three-minute midpoint, F stops and holds the ball. Count both quartets and exchange them by the inspected route; restart only with all children placed. Keep this within the clock. If a ball enters your group's space, call stop—children do not chase it.

Notice observable control and comfort; privately offer “Want it closer or want to watch once?” Keep support visible and interruptible.

## Preparation notes — outside player time

**T−12 to −7:** H inspects the hitting sector, bat/tee/protection and waiting spot. F/G inspect footing and actual throw, roll, miss and collection paths. Mark parallel independent lanes only if errant balls cannot enter the neighboring lane or its waiting space. Each lane's adult stands at its own target end; nobody throws across lanes. Check separation from hitting and the protected route between areas. Use two flat bases for first targets; remaining bases can stay stored. Confirm water, current local conditions and approved care arrangements. Select the one-lane option before intake if there is any doubt; inspect its ball-free waiting spaces too.

**T−7 to −3:** Walk the assignments and stop/collection routes. Demonstrate one cue and easier retry for each activity. F/G teach back places, boundaries, stop and fallback; H verifies understanding. Confirm no spare adult exists during the two-lane plan.

**T−3 to 0:** Set the neutral pairs, finish equipment checks, and accept children only when ready. Early arrivals remain with guardians. If early access fails, keep guardians responsible while preparing; count any delay against the scheduled session, remove complete early rounds as necessary, and preserve 43–50 for closure. If safe setup/coverage cannot be established, postpone.

**Support without labels:** Quietly tell the older beginner, within the supervised group, “Everyone gets a starting version that works today. Want the close roll first?” Give genuine turns, not a permanent helper job. Offer the same choices to everyone. For the two players who turn away from catches, use predictable ground rolls and no child receiving duty. Check glare, glove comfort and willingness; catching airborne throws is not required today. Previous experience earns an optional challenge, not faster feeds or a leadership burden.

**Opportunity check:** These are estimates, not quotas. Each pair gets six tee minutes: reserve one minute for extra setup/margin, leaving 300 seconds ÷ 75 seconds per complete two-swing visit = four visits, or about four swings each. Start-to-start between a child's visits is about 150 seconds; their partner's visit occupies about 75 seconds. Rotate who starts.

For each six-minute field block, allow 80 seconds for demonstration, closure/retrieval and margin: 280 ÷ 25 ÷ four ≈ 2–3 complete attempts each. Each child fields in four rounds: roughly 8–11 attempts, about 100 seconds between their turn starts before extra stops. In the one-lane fallback, each three-minute block reserves 55 seconds for demonstration, collection/exchange and margin: 125 ÷ 25 = five turns for four children. Across four blocks, expect about 4–5 ball attempts per child, with the same roughly 100-second turn spacing. Rotate the extra retry fairly. This is a modest introduction, not ample repeated fielding practice; ball-free movements do not inflate that count. Revisit the sequence next practice.

Time the first full cycles. If slower, shorten the roll/send distance and simplify to stop-and-roll; never hurry feeds or remove stops. Use spare turns for an easier retry, and report actual opportunities afterward. The final game is simultaneous, so every child fits its three minutes without a serial queue.

**Arrivals/interruptions:** Late children stay with guardians until a stopped round change; their boundary demonstration replaces attempts. If an adult is late, do not open their station: retain guardian care until approved coverage and briefing are ready, shortening the activity clock. Recheck turns, comfort and conditions at stopped changes. Necessary water or care may reduce attempts or end play.

**After every handoff:** Allow two adult minutes for teardown, then three for one observed success, one wait/setup difficulty and one next-practice adjustment. Increase teardown time if needed.
