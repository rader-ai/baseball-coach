# 42-minute coach card

**Today:** nine children, three mixed-readiness groups of three (A/B/C), one beginner in each. Everyone gets opportunities to control a ground ball, send it toward a first-base marker, and hit from a tee. Plan for **four ground-ball attempts with up to four sends, and four tee swings per child**. These are opportunities, not promised catches, throws, or contacts. This is introductory practice; the single fielding lane leaves long gaps.

**Adults stay at their jobs:** head coach runs the only ground-ball lane; helper 1 runs the separate hitting sector; helper 2 supervises three children in the protected ball-free area. Each adult keeps their own three in view. Two waiting children at either ball activity stand at marked spots outside ball/bat paths. Use gloves, soft balls, cones, flat bases, tee, bat and the supplied required helmets; keep unused balls with adults.

**Ground balls:** adult stands at the target end beside a flat “first” marker, rolls toward one ready child, then receives the child's return toward first. This straight route rehearses control and a send, not the angles or decisions of a game play. Cue: **“Look, then send.”** Start close with a gentle roll and an underhand send; increase only the roll's challenge when ready. No child receiver. Stop the lane to recover a miss before the next turn.

**Tee:** only the called, helmeted batter holds the bat. Helper stands outside the swing arc and ball path; nobody catches behind the batter. Cue: **“Look at the ball.”** Offer two comfortable swings, resetting the tee between them. Easier: adjust tee height and use a shorter swing. Park the bat before stopped collection; restart only when everyone is back and the sector is empty.

**Ball-free area:** helper 2 leads walking to flat bases, stopping in a balanced stance, then side steps in personal spaces. Easier: walk or use seated pointing. This is supervised movement, not throwing or catching.

**“STOP!”** means no swings/throws, hold balls, park bat, look at adult. Anyone calls it. Collect only in a closed lane; count before coach-directed swaps. Adults confirm clear spaces and readiness before restart. Water remains available throughout. Pain/fatigue stops throwing. Care needs, unsafe conditions or lost coverage stop play; use your confirmed arrangements and resume only with safe coverage. End early if necessary. Supervision continues until every authorized handoff is complete.

## Brief helper instructions

**Helper 1 — tee, all six five-minute visits.** Keep the same child order on each group's second visit. Allow 30 seconds to show the task, then three approximately 75-second turns with two swings each; the remaining 45 seconds is margin/closure. A turn includes helmet readiness, swings, tee reset, bat parking, stopped retrieval and child change. Watch whether the child sees the ball; demonstrate a shorter swing and offer another try. No practice swings in the queue. If retrieval or instruction makes this pace unrealistic, give each child one swing per visit; keep every child's turn and report the reduced count. Never rush a swing or retrieve into live hitting.

**Helper 2 — movement, all six visits.** Keep your three inside the protected area, in personal spaces, with no balls or bats. Demonstrate the walk/stop route, then let children choose walking or side steps. Keep them out of both ball activities. Lead your group through swaps only after the coach's stop and signal; count all three before restarting. Signal care needs to the head coach, retaining supervision. You are not an extra ball-lane coach or a spare supervisor while your group is active.

## Preparation and honest participation notes

**T−12 to −7:** coach marks the single fielding route and safe waiting spots; helper 1 sets the hitting/waiting sector and checks equipment; helper 2 sets the protected movement area and water access. Confirm current local conditions and applicable league requirements using your established procedures. **T−7 to −3:** walk the routes; show cue, easier retry and stopped retrieval; both helpers teach back their roles. **T−3 to 0:** rehearse a complete cycle, finalize groups and verify readiness. Guardians retain children until accepted at intake. If setup or coverage is not ready, delay play; do not run an unstaffed activity. A late child stays with their guardian until the 13–15 pause; their boundary briefing replaces later turns as needed.

**Ground-ball arithmetic:** each five-minute visit reserves 30 seconds for demonstration and 60 for pauses/closure. That leaves 210 seconds: six complete **35-second** cycles, or two per child. Each cycle includes readiness, adult roll, control attempt, child's send if possible, recovery/reset and change. Two visits yield four fielding opportunities and up to four actual sends. Record completed actions separately; a miss without a send is not a throw.

**Tee arithmetic:** 300 − 30 demonstration − 45 margin = 225 seconds; three **75-second** two-swing turns give two swings each per visit, four overall. Contact is not guaranteed. These cycle lengths are planning estimates, to be checked during preparation and the first live turns.

**Spacing across the whole rotation:** using the same child order, ground-ball starts recur about **1:45 apart within a visit**, but the last attempt in one visit to the first in the next is about **17:15 for A/B and 16:15 for C**. Tee swings within a turn are roughly 20 seconds apart; between visits the gap is about **17:40 for A and 18:40 for B/C**. Pauses can push target-skill gaps toward 20 minutes or longer; these are estimates, not maximum waits.

Tracing both ball activities together gives a longest planned interval without either a ground-ball attempt or tee swing of approximately **11½–13 minutes for A/B and 10½–12 minutes for C**, depending on turn position. C also waits until its 8–13 tee visit for its first ball activity. The intervals include an entire supervised movement visit and transitions, not just standing idle. At the fielding lane, each child waits through two other 35-second turns (about 70 seconds); at the tee, the last child waits through two 75-second turns (about 2½ minutes), plus setup. Movement, watching, the adult's rolls and the final game add **zero child ball attempts**.

**If slower:** do one complete fielding round per visit before considering extras. Budgeting just that round gives two field-and-send opportunities per child over the session, roughly 18–19 minutes between them, plus delays. The one-swing tee fallback gives two swings per child, also roughly 18–19 minutes apart. Neither fallback supplies much repetition. Tell families the session became a brief introduction; do not claim the original counts, add a second lane, or borrow from safe transitions or water. For more repetition next time, devote a session mainly to fielding or secure additional genuinely safe space.

After all pickups, allow two minutes for teardown and three for the adult review: one success, one wait/setup problem, one adjustment. Increase teardown time if needed. This is an original coaching plan, not a verified league curriculum; local rules and real-time conditions remain yours to apply.
