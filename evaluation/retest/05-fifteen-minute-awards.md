Make the awards **ten equally warm thank-yous**, with no purchases, rankings, or attendance prizes. Two are delivered quietly within the 15-minute program. Families who missed games receive the same welcome and recognition.

Before the event, privately check each child's name pronunciation and preferred format: seated public recognition, a quiet thank-you with their guardian, or recognition at an agreed later time. For [Players I and J], plan quiet recognition without announcing their preference or calling them forward. Offer these choices to everyone. No child has to speak, stand, pose, chant, or touch anyone.

Use an accessible seating area with clear routes. For the plan below, guardians remain with and supervise their own children throughout; confirm that arrangement and the league's requirements in advance. You present. If you instead have custody of the group and cannot give quiet acknowledgments while supervising everyone, arrange another approved adult to supervise the group, or agree a later recognition with each caregiver. Do not silently assume a second coach or ask an unapproved spectator to take charge.

Seat the two quiet-recognition families near an edge you can reach while remaining visible. Keep everyone in the same gathering area. Use the same roughly 15–20 seconds of words for each child, within a 30-second slot. Public recipients can stay seated, so there is no procession or stage transition.

| Time | Run of show and sample words |
|---|---|
| 0:00–2:00 | Welcome and settle. “Thank you for being part of our season. Today we're thanking every teammate. You can stay seated, listen, wave, or pass on speaking.” |
| 2:00–3:00 | One brief team reflection. “This season gave us chances to learn baseball, try things, and share time with teammates.” Add one true team memory if you have one; otherwise use the statement as written. |
| 3:00–4:00 | Explain the next short activity and allow settling time: “Think with your family about one thing you enjoyed this season. You can talk quietly or just think.” Use this transition to reach the quiet-recognition families without announcing them. |
| 4:00–5:00 | Give [Player I] and [Player J] one quiet 30-second recognition each, with their guardians nearby, while other children remain with their guardians. If a second approved adult is providing group coverage, that adult supervises during this block. No public applause is requested for these two. |
| 5:00–9:00 | Eight public recognitions, [Players A–H], 30 seconds each. Deliver the brief thank-you, allow a wave or brief applause if welcomed, then move to the next child. Children remain seated unless they prefer otherwise. |
| 9:00–12:00 | Optional memories, including regrouping time: “Would anyone like to share one thing they enjoyed? Passing is fine.” Take at most three brief responses. If no one wants to speak, thank everyone and allow quiet family reflection; do not call on reluctant children. |
| 12:00–13:00 | “Families and helpers, thank you for supporting these children in the ways you could. Every teammate belongs in today's thank-you.” Offer an optional shared wave. |
| 13:00–15:00 | Close and complete any required guardian handoffs. Offer quiet goodbyes and keep routes clear. Continue supervision after minute 15 for any child whose required handoff is incomplete. |

That is 15 minutes. Individual recognition occupies five minutes: two quiet slots total one minute and eight public slots total four. Settling, movement into place, reflection, and closing have their own time.

Use one of these recognition lines, choosing an observed example only when it really happened:

- “[Player A], thank you for being part of our team. I noticed you [returned our practice balls]. That helped everyone get ready for the next activity.”
- “[Player B], thank you for being part of our team. I remember you [encouraged a teammate after a missed ball]. Thank you for that kindness.”
- “[Player C], thank you for being part of our team. I saw you [try the ready-position cue at practice]. I enjoyed practicing with you.”
- For any child without an individual observation: “[Player D], thank you for being part of our team. I'm glad we got to share baseball this season.”

Use the same pattern and warmth for [Players E–H]. Quiet words for [Player I] or [Player J] can be exactly the same: “[Player I], thank you for being part of our team. I'm glad we got to share baseball this season.” Add a true observation if available. Different delivery preferences do not require different award value.

Avoid “most improved,” “best,” jokes at a child's expense, or praise based on perfect attendance. Missing games does not require an explanation or a lesser award.

If a child is absent from the ceremony, reserve the same recognition for an agreed later caregiver handoff; a draft note using the same words is also possible. Do not explain the absence publicly. Shorten the recognition block if needed rather than giving the remaining children longer, more elaborate awards. Any later arrangement is explicit, not an unnoticed extension of today's program. No photo or outgoing message is included in this plan.
