# 45-minute practice: eight five-year-old beginners

Keep one visible group for the entire practice. Start with your permitted solo coverage, then bring the approved assistant into that same group at minute 10. Today's goals are **stop a slow rolling ball** and **travel through first base**. The final game uses ready positions and controlled stops.

Use cones, one flat base, and one soft ball; secure the second ball as a spare. No batting is planned. Bring the normal league emergency kit/contact access and ensure drinking water is available. This assumes you can inspect and mark a clear area before accepting children, the designated guardian's care role follows your league procedure, and current field/weather conditions permit play. Your supplied solo-supervision permission is accepted; this plan does not establish permission for any other league.

## Before players: 12 adult minutes

| Time before start | Your job |
|---|---|
| −12 to −7 | Inspect footing and nearby traffic. Mark one short roll lane, a waiting line beside and outside that lane, and a visible water area. Mark a separate walking/running route through the flat base with clear space beyond to slow down and a separate return route. These routes are used sequentially. |
| −7 to −3 | Place the assistant card below at the equipment spot. Rehearse the stop and collection sequence yourself. Secure both balls. Confirm the guardian care/handoff procedure, emergency access, authorized pickup process, and weather plan. You cannot brief an absent assistant; that happens at 10–14. |
| −3 to 0 | Confirm the whole area is ready and your designated guardian is present. Early children remain with their guardians until you accept each handoff. Stand where you can see the entrance, waiting line, and water area. |

If setup cannot finish before intake, keep children with guardians until the safe area is ready. If the start becomes five minutes late, use the exact 40-minute version below; do not finish setup while supervising a live drill.

**Stop signal:** anyone can call “FREEZE.” Children stop their feet, hold any ball, and look to you. Nobody fetches a ball until you explicitly open collection. You alone authorize restart after checking all eight children and a clear lane. Water remains available throughout; a child drinking stays in your visible supervised area.

## Player clock: exactly 45 minutes

| Elapsed time | What happens, including teaching and movement | Adult coverage |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Accept handoffs and count eight. Show waiting/water places, the lane, and “FREEZE”; let everyone practice the signal. Explain “We take turns; everyone gets to play.” | You supervise all eight. The designated guardian has only the agreed care role. |
| 3–6 | **Move and freeze.** First 30 seconds: demonstrate walking and stopping in individual spaces. Then 2 minutes of walking/side-step calls; last 30 seconds: freeze and walk to waiting marks. Cue: “Move, then freeze.” Easier: stay in place and point/move arms. | You face the whole group; balls stay secured. |
| 6–10 | **First slow rolls.** 45 seconds: demonstrate a gentle roll, moving behind it, stopping it with hands, and rolling it back. Cue: “Get behind the ball.” About 2½ minutes: four short turns, one child in the lane at a time. Others watch for the ready signal. Final 45 seconds: secure ball, count, and move to visible water/rest spots. Start with the other four after the assistant briefing. | You roll from the target end with the waiting group in view. No child receiver behind you and no loose second ball. |
| 10–14 | **Water and late-assistant briefing, with all play stopped.** 10–11: assistant reads the card while you supervise drinking/rest. 11–12: point out group/coach positions, the roll lane, and the closed running route. 12–13: demonstrate the cue and slower/closer retry. 13–14: assistant points to waiting places, demonstrates stop/collection, and teaches back when to call you. Count eight and explicitly transfer the roll-leader role. Children remain together, visible and equipment-free; no station opens during this briefing. | You retain group supervision throughout. Assistant takes responsibility only after the teach-back is clear. |
| 14–23 | **Roll and stop.** 14–15: assistant gives a 30–45-second demonstration and invites a child to show readiness. 15–22: short turns, starting with the four who have not tried, then cycle through as time allows. Each turn includes ready signal, one or two slow rolls, return, and walking to the end of the line. 22–23: stop, secure ball, and count. If a ball escapes, stop everyone before the adult collects it. | Assistant runs the one roll activity; you manage the waiting children and water within that same group. No simultaneous running. |
| 23–25 | **Change to base running.** All balls secured. Walk the route together, including slowing beyond the flat base and returning outside the outbound lane; take positions. This is the transition, not extra drill time. | You lead the walk. Assistant keeps the group together and checks the return route. |
| 25–33 | **Through first.** 25–26: you demonstrate and say “Run past first, then slow down.” 26–32: one child at a time travels the clear lane; release the next only after the previous child clears the route. Begin at a walk; running is optional. 32–33: last child returns, everyone freezes, and you count. | You give the start signal and watch the base/slow-down area; assistant supervises the waiting line and return entry. Both see the single group. |
| 33–35 | **Water and reset.** All children in the visible rest area. Take 30 seconds to check comfort and whether the next game needs a walking/in-place version, then move to personal spaces. | Assistant stays with the group while you confirm the game area; do not leave the area. |
| 35–41 | **Final fun game — Baseball Statues.** 35–36: demonstrate three calls: “ready” = comfortable ready pose, “first” = point to first base, “freeze” = still statue. 36–40: call them in changing order; invite children to invent one safe funny statue. Everyone plays, with no elimination or winner. 40–41: final silly statue, then walk to the wrap-up area. Easier: seated poses or pointing only. | You lead and see everyone; assistant watches spacing and supports a child who wants a supervised pause. Bats absent; balls secured. |
| 41–45 | **Wrap-up and handoff.** 41–42: optional “What helped you stop the ball?” and truthful appreciation such as “You waited for the ready signal.” Optional wave/cheer. 42–45: begin individual authorized guardian handoffs and check each child off. | One coach remains with all uncollected children while the other manages handoff. Supervision continues beyond minute 45 until every handoff is complete. |

For rolling, a slower, straight roll from closer is the easier retry; only offer a gentle side roll once the child wants it and controls the straight roll. Watch whether the child moves into the path and stops the ball; do not add posture instructions. For running, shorten the route or walk it again if “through” is unclear. A modest next challenge is starting on the spoken “go,” not a race. Turn counts are planning estimates, never a reason to rush.

## Small card for the approved assistant

> **TODAY: roll leader 14–23; whole-group support afterward.** Read 10–11; field walkthrough/teach-back 11–14 before taking the role.
>
> **Eight children, one group.** You stand at the roll end; one ready child faces you. Others wait beside and outside the lane with the head coach. One live soft ball; spare secured. No running while rolling is live.
>
> **Show:** gentle roll → move behind it → stop → roll back. **Say:** “Get behind the ball.” The cue gives the child a useful place to move; it is not a guarantee.
>
> **Watch:** did the child get into the roll's path and stop it? If the ball goes past, check the surface/feed first. Demonstrate a slower, closer straight roll, retry, and see whether stopping becomes manageable. Praise a real action: “You moved behind that one.” Change one demand at a time.
>
> **Turns:** start with the four who did not try at 6–10, then cycle. One/two rolls, return, rotate. Save 22–23 for stopping and counting.
>
> **FREEZE:** stop feet, hold ball. Everyone waits while the adult retrieves; no rolls during collection or crossings. Anyone can stop; only head coach restarts. Stop for distress, pain, fatigue, unsafe movement, lost coverage, or weather concerns.
>
> **Ask without leaving:** call the head coach for a care need, unclear instruction, unsuccessful adjustment, or equipment problem. Do not walk away from children. For base running, supervise the waiting line/return entry; for Statues, watch spacing. Head coach calls transitions.
>
> **After all pickups:** bring one observed success and one suggested change to the three-minute debrief.

## Interruptions and shorter options

- **Assistant arrives after minute 10:** keep 10–14 as supervised water/rest, then you lead 14–23 alone with one child rolling at a time. Do not hand over a station on arrival. At 23–27, stop all play for the four-minute card/walkthrough/teach-back; use 27–29 for the route walk/demo, 29–32 for short base-travel turns, and 32–33 for stopping/counting. If they miss that pause too, finish solo using the same whole-group activities, provided the supplied permission and care coverage still hold. A briefing that is still unclear means they do not independently lead.
- **Briefing needs two extra minutes:** extend the stopped block to 16; roll 16–23 (one-minute demo, five minutes of turns, one-minute closure). Everything from 23 onward stays as written. For longer delay, retain solo leadership rather than borrowing water or transitions.
- **Care/restroom:** call FREEZE, secure the ball, keep all other children visible, and use the supplied league-approved guardian handoff procedure. Resume only after coverage/count are clear. If you would have to leave the group, stop the session until approved coverage is restored.
- **Late child:** their guardian retains care until the next stopped intake window at 10–14, 23–25, or 33–35. Accept the handoff and show boundaries/stop signal there; spend less time on attempts or extend the pause and trim the next drill.
- **Five-minute routine reduction:** use 0–3 welcome, 3–6 movement, 6–10 first rolls, 10–14 briefing/water, 14–20 rolls, 20–22 route transition, 22–29 base travel, 29–31 water, 31–37 Statues, 37–40 wrap/handoff. This totals 40 minutes. If the assistant's arrival differs, retain solo leadership until a stopped briefing fits.
- **Weather/fatigue:** current local conditions and league policy decide whether to modify, stop, or cancel. In the normal 45-minute clock, replace 25–33 running with supervised rest and gentle in-place movement when appropriate; offer seated Statues. If conditions are unsafe, stop and follow the shelter/handoff plan immediately, regardless of the final game.

After the last authorized handoff, take **three adult minutes** for one success, one difficulty, and one adjustment; make a solo note if the assistant has left. Then allow **two adult minutes** to collect cones/base and pack remaining equipment.

These are original coaching/timing suggestions. Developmental guidance supports brief demonstrations and play suited to readiness, not a guaranteed outcome or an ideal 45-minute dose. [AAP sports readiness guidance](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/sports/Pages/Is-Your-Child-Ready-for-Sports.aspx), accessed October 11, 2026. League identity, local equipment rules, and live weather were not supplied or independently verified; confirm them before running the plan.
