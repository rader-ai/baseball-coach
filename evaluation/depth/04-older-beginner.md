Seventy-five minutes is enough for a useful beginner practice, but I would not try to cover every baseball skill. Nine- and ten-year-old beginners still need easy starting tasks and time to repeat them. Today's two goals are **send a ball into open space from a tee** and **stop a slow rolling ball under control**. Save pitching, catching a pitcher, fly balls, relays, sliding, and live defensive decisions for later sessions.

This is a runnable draft for 12 players, three approved coaches, your separated safe sectors, soft balls, cones, and one usable batting setup. It assumes the batting setup includes a manageable permitted bat and properly fitting required batting helmet. Confirm that before opening it; if either is absent, Coach A runs another roll-and-stop lane in the same time slots. The spare tee stays stored: two tees do not create a second usable hitting area. Local conditions and rules are permitted as supplied; coaches must still respond if conditions change. These are planning suggestions, not league requirements or a certification of field safety.

**Before player minute 0: allow 15 adult minutes.**

| Time | Work and responsibility |
|---|---|
| −15 to −9 | A inspects and marks the single batting sector; B and C mark their rolling lanes. Together check that even errant hits cannot enter another station, the water/waiting areas, or the walking route. Check footing, gear, emergency access, and the league's shelter, care/restroom, and pickup procedures. |
| −9 to −4 | Walk the three cards below. Each coach points out player places, shows the cue and easier retry, then teaches back the stop/retrieval procedure. A controls the clock and group changes. Anyone can call a stop. |
| −4 to 0 | Set soft balls and cones, store unused bats/tee, confirm required equipment, and count available adults. Guardians retain early arrivals until coaches explicitly accept them. A coach accepting a child stops setup work and supervises that child. |

If early field access is unavailable, use player minutes 0–15 for accepted-player supervision, safe setup, and briefing, then run the 45-minute version below from minutes 15–60. A supervises all accepted children in a visible, safe waiting area while B/C prepare; do not do this if local coverage requirements or the site prevent it. Do not hide setup inside a warm-up. Shorten or postpone if safe coverage cannot be established.

**People, places, and equipment.** Label groups Red, Blue, and Gold, four children each. Coaches stay at stations; groups move together only at A's instruction. A runs Tee Contact, B runs Roll and Stop, C runs Stop and Deliver. Each coach supervises their entire group, including waiting children. There is no spare coach during rotations.

Use one active child at a time in each lane. Mark four waiting places outside swing reach and every likely ball path. At the tee, the waiting area must be outside the batted-ball sector and possible stray-ball paths, not simply a few steps behind the batter. A stands outside full bat reach, with the hitter and waiting players in view. No child catcher or live fielders. B/C stand at their lane's adult feed end, with their waiting group beside and outside the lane; rolls travel only along that lane. Water sits in a safe, visible place accessible without crossing live lanes. Stop the activity to escort or supervise access if needed.

Use **“STOP—hold balls, park bats”** as the literal stop signal. A batter sets the bat down in the adult-designated spot; no one carries it to retrieve. Coaches confirm all equipment still, then authorize collection within their closed lanes. Stray balls outside a sector stay there until all affected stations are stopped. Close → secure → collect → count → move on A's signal → reset → restart. No simultaneous hitting and retrieval, no unsignaled swaps, and no practice swings while waiting.

For a program-specific equipment example, Little League requires protective NOCSAE batting helmets in practice and prohibits on-deck bat handling in Tee Ball, Minor, and Major divisions. This does not establish which organization governs this group; use its current requirements. [Little League Safety Code, checked October 11, 2026](https://www.littleleague.org/playing-rules/appendices/appendix-b/).

**Player clock: 75 minutes total.** Water is available throughout; the scheduled breaks are reminders.

| Minutes | Activity and adult jobs |
|---|---|
| 0–6 | All coaches accept/count their four players. A gives the two goals, demonstrates STOP, and points out water and boundaries; B/C help children show the stop response. First three minutes intake, next three instructions. |
| 6–12 | A leads comfortable walking, easy jogging, side steps, and freezing in individual spaces; B/C watch spacing. First minute demonstration, four minutes movement, last minute group assignment. Cue: “Move, then freeze.” Easier: walk or point/move seated as appropriate. |
| 12–25 | Round 1: Red–A, Blue–B, Gold–C. Each 13-minute station includes 1 minute demonstration, 10 minutes attempts/rest/reset/retrieval, and 2 minutes final collection/count. |
| 25–28 | All stop. Water, count, and escorted group change: Red–B, Blue–C, Gold–A. Allow three minutes; do not start a lane until its coach confirms readiness. |
| 28–41 | Round 2, same 13-minute internal budget. Rotate which child goes first. |
| 41–45 | Stop, water, visible coach check, and group change: Red–C, Blue–A, Gold–B. A checks comfort and attention while everyone is stopped. |
| 45–58 | Round 3, same internal budget. No new skill added because time remains. |
| 58–62 | Close all stations, park bats, water, and walk groups into three isolated rolling lanes for the final game. A's group uses the now-closed batting sector only after equipment is secured and it is safely reconfigured. |
| 62–70 | **Final fun game: Team Delivery.** One-minute demo, six minutes play, one minute collection. Each coach rolls gently to one ready child; the child stops it and rolls it back through a broad cone gate toward the coach. Count the whole group's completed deliveries together; there is no winner, elimination, speed race, or penalty for a miss. Waiting children can signal readiness or count aloud from their safe places. Change who starts. Easier: closer straight roll and wider gate, or point to the gate while the adult demonstrates. Children may choose a supervised pause. |
| 70–75 | Positive wrap-up and pickup: optional “What helped you stop the ball?”, appreciate an observed action, optional cheer, then controlled guardian handoffs. Coaches retain supervision of remaining children beyond minute 75 until every handoff is complete. |

**Coach A's card: Tee Contact — each rotation, 13 minutes.**

Purpose: controlled contact into an empty sector. Equipment: one tee, soft balls, permitted manageable bat, fitted required helmet, cone boundary; spare tee and bats stored. Only the called batter holds a bat. A adjusts the tee with the bat parked, then moves fully outside swing reach before inviting the swing. Everyone else stays at the waiting markers with empty hands.

Show for 30–45 seconds: comfortable stance and a controlled swing into open space. Say **“Send the ball into that space.”** The broad target gives the child a clear purpose. Observe whether the bat reaches the ball and the swing stays controlled; do not prescribe elbow angles or infer eye fixation from head position. If contact misses, first check tee height/location and bat manageability. Park bat, adjust one thing, demonstrate, retry, and see whether contact becomes easier. Easier: comfortable tee adjustment or larger suitable soft ball if available. Harder: choose a broad direction only when control and willingness support it.

Turns: one short turn of up to two comfortable swings, then bat parked and A directs retrieval before the next child. If only one ball is available, retrieve after each swing. Plan roughly 60–90 seconds per child's turn including helmet check/reset/collection; ten working minutes may fit one or two passes through four children, not a guaranteed quota. Reduce swings rather than rush checks. Rotate first batter next round. Encourage truthfully: “You parked the bat before collecting.” If batting gear is missing, replace with Card B's activity for the same group and clock.

**Coach B's card: Roll and Stop — each rotation, 13 minutes.**

Purpose: move into the path of a slow routine roll and stop it. One child is at the receiving marker; three watch from outside the lane. B rolls only after a ready signal, waits for control, then invites a gentle roll back. Say **“Move behind the ball.”** Demonstrate comfortable movement and hands/glove low enough for this slow roll; do not demand body blocking of bad hops.

Observe movement toward the roll and whether it is controlled. If it passes by, check uneven ground and feed pace before changing the child. Give a closer, straighter roll and demonstrate once; check the retry. Easier: shorter slow roll. Harder: a small known side roll, without increasing speed too. Give two or three rolls per brief turn; allow reset and rotate all four. A loose ball means stop before retrieval. Encourage: “You moved into its path.” Children without a usable glove can use suitable soft-ball hand stops if comfortable.

**Coach C's card: Stop and Deliver — each rotation, 13 minutes.**

Purpose: repeat the same stopping goal with a simple destination. C rolls slowly to one ready child. After stopping, the child points to a broad cone gate and rolls back to C through it. This is not a pitching or overhand-throwing station. Three children wait outside the lane; C is the only receiver.

Say **“Stop it, then find the gate.”** Demonstrate the pause so there is no rushed return. Observe possession before return and general direction. If the child rushes, check whether the two actions were understood; demonstrate stopping alone, then invite the return separately. Easier: stop only and let C collect on a closed lane, or widen the gate. Harder: choose one of two safe adjacent gates toward the same adult end; do not add speed or a live runner. Give two or three rolls per turn, with resets. Encourage: “You controlled it before sending it back.”

**Shared card footer for A/B/C.** Stop for pain, fatigue, distress, a child leaving the area, unsafe behavior, gear trouble, or changed conditions. Pain/fatigue stops throwing; never use a technical change to work through it. For care/restroom needs, call A without leaving the group; stop all play and implement the agreed coverage/guardian procedure. A batter's coach cannot simultaneously cover another station. No restart until adults confirm coverage, child counts, clear lanes, and understanding. Bring one observation and one adjustment to the afterbrief.

**Shorter version and interruption budgets.**

For a planned **45-minute attention-friendly version**, use 0–6 welcome/stop practice; 6–9 easy movement; 9–17 round 1; 17–19 water/swap; 19–27 round 2; 27–30 water/swap; 30–38 round 3; 38–42 Team Delivery; 42–45 wrap-up/handoff. Each eight-minute station uses one minute demonstration, six minutes turns with resets/retrieval, and one minute close/count. The four-minute game includes its demo and collection. Use identical cards with fewer turns; do not promise every child a second turn.

If attention fades during the 75-minute plan, finish safely at the next stop and spend the next **four minutes** securing equipment, water, and regrouping; **four minutes** on Team Delivery; **three minutes** on wrap-up/handoff. For example, a decision at minute 41 ends the planned program at minute 52. Some stations may remain unvisited; note that for next time. Fatigue, distress, or unsafe conditions may require ending immediately instead of playing the game.

Late players stay with guardians until the next scheduled stopped break. Use up to two minutes of that break/next block for accepted handoff and boundary/stop demonstration; remove attempts to pay for it. No coach leaves a live station for intake. If an assistant is late or leaves, stop/recount/secure first: no unstaffed station. With two approved adults and league-permitted coverage, use one visible whole-group roll-and-stop activity, one active child at a time, with the other adult supporting the waiting group; retain the next water and final-game/closing blocks, reduce turns. A late approved assistant needs a four-minute card briefing/teach-back during a stopped period before rotations reopen; subtract it from the next activity. With one adult, or inadequate coverage, keep children supervised under the league procedure and postpone/end the session.

For a routine conditions change that still permits activity, use the shorter sequence and shade/rest. If policy requires a weather stop or cancellation, stop and follow the shelter/handoff plan; the game is optional in that safety stop. Do not send children home unsupervised.

After the last guardian handoff, take **three adult minutes** for one success, one setup difficulty, and one adjustment, then **five minutes** for teardown. Allow longer if needed. A useful anonymous note is “offer closer roll first” or “Gold starts batting next time,” not a ranking or diagnosis. This plan has not been tested at your field.
