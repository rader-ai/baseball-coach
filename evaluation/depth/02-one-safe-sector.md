# 50-minute practice: ten seven/eight-year-olds, one ball sector

**Three simultaneous stations are not workable with this staffing and space.** The uncle is not cleared to coach, and a coach attending the batter cannot supervise another station. One safe ball sector also cannot hold simultaneous hitting and throwing. Run throwing, tee hitting, and base travel sequentially with all ten children in one supervised group. You still cover all three activities, with the explanations, waits, retrieval, and transitions counted below.

Name the two approved adults **Head Coach** and **Assistant**. The uncle remains a spectator in the permitted spectator area; do not assign him a station, waiting-group supervision, helmet fitting, retrieval, or restroom coverage. His willingness does not confer clearance. Little League, if that is your organization, requires volunteer approval before service; your actual league's process controls. [Little League volunteer guidance](https://www.littleleague.org/university/articles/what-you-need-to-know-about-the-little-league-volunteer-application-process/), accessed October 11, 2026.

Assume the sector has enough clear space for likely errant balls and a waiting area safely outside both ball paths and bat reach, and that both coaches can see the whole group. If that waiting area does not exist, **omit live hitting and throwing** and use the ball-free replacement below. Cones mark boundaries; they do not stop balls. Confirm weather/field conditions, local gear requirements, emergency arrangements, and the approved care/pickup procedure before intake.

Use the supplied tee, one bat, inspected correctly fitted required helmets, soft balls, and cones. One/two balls are used per turn; all others stay secured. Use a cone **gate** for base travel; do not ask children to step on a cone. Water stays available in a visible safe location throughout.

## Adult setup/prebrief: 12 minutes before intake

| Before start | Adult work |
|---|---|
| −12 to −7 | Head Coach inspects the sector and marks a broad empty target end plus a start point. Assistant checks bat, tee, helmets, and balls, and marks waiting/water places outside full swing reach and foreseeable ball paths. Identify a closed-lane retrieval route. Put all gear at the controlled equipment spot. |
| −7 to −3 | Walk the card below: first minute goals/roles; second minute actual positions, ball direction, and stop/collection; third minute one cue, demonstration, and easier retry; fourth minute Assistant teaches back everyone's locations, when a ball can be collected, and when to stop or call for help. Correct uncertainties before opening. |
| −3 to 0 | Check local safeguarding/care coverage and emergency access, current conditions, and the clear waiting area. Early arrivals remain with guardians until accepted handoff. |

**Controls:** “FREEZE” means stop, hold balls, and park the bat with the Head Coach. Anyone can stop. The Head Coach closes the sector, secures gear, authorizes collection, counts children, moves the group, and restarts only after the Assistant confirms everyone is placed and ready. No throws or swings during retrieval. Only the called batter holds the bat; no practice swings in the waiting area and no child catcher. The Head Coach stays entirely outside full swing reach while a child swings and approaches the tee only after the bat is parked.

If applicable, Little League's Safety Code requires batting helmets at practice and prohibits on-deck bat handling in Tee Ball, Minor, and Major divisions. These controls are retained in this plan; verify your own league/division's full requirements. Soft balls do not remove the need for protective gear or separation. [Little League Safety Code](https://www.littleleague.org/playing-rules/appendices/appendix-b/), accessed October 11, 2026.

## Full player clock: exactly 50 minutes

| Time | Runnable activity and included teaching/transition | Roles and waiting children |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 | Accept handoffs, count ten, point out water/waiting/sector boundaries. Demonstrate FREEZE, parked bat, and closed-lane collection. Have children show the waiting position and stop signal. | Head Coach speaks; Assistant keeps everyone in the safe waiting area and handles accepted arrivals. No live equipment. |
| 4–8 | **Ready movement.** 4–4:45 demonstrate walking, side-stepping, and freezing in personal spaces. 4:45–7:15 play movement calls. 7:15–8 freeze and walk to waiting marks. Cue: “Move, then freeze.” Easier: pointing or in-place movement. | Head Coach leads; Assistant watches spacing. Balls and bat secured. |
| 8–10 | **Water and throwing setup.** Children drink in the visible rest area; Head Coach confirms target direction and retrieves two soft balls. Assistant keeps all ten together. Move back to waiting marks only on the Head Coach's signal. | No warm-up throws or independent activity during the transition. |
| 10–19 | **Activity 1: step and throw.** 10–11 includes a 45-second easy demonstration: identify broad empty target, take a comfortable step, throw, then wait. Cue: “Step toward your target.” 11–18: ten brief turns at roughly 40 seconds per child, including one/two easy throws, stopping, adult collection, and reset. Give everyone a first opportunity before extra turns; estimates are not quotas. 18–19: close the lane, collect remaining balls, count ten, secure balls. | Head Coach controls the active child and target sector. Assistant supervises the nine waiting children, who watch whether the thrower identifies the target; they hold no balls. Head Coach retrieves only after the sector is closed, with the active child back at the waiting mark. |
| 19–22 | **Water, helmet checks, and tee conversion.** 19–20 water. 20–21 Head Coach places tee while Assistant keeps everyone in the rest area. 21–22 confirm fitted helmet for the first batter, show the safe route to the batter's spot, and confirm the waiting group. If gear fitting needs more time, use time from hitting attempts. | No live throws. Assistant stays with the group; Head Coach controls all batting gear. |
| 22–36 | **Activity 2: tee contact.** 22–23: Head Coach demonstrates with every child safely outside reach, parks the bat, and says “Send the ball into that space.” 23–34: rotate ten batters, allowing about one minute per child for helmet check, taking the bat, one/two swings, parking, adult collection, and return. A miss uses a turn too; offer an easier setup without hurrying. 34–36: finish safely, park bat, close lane, collect loose balls, count ten. | Head Coach tends one batter; Assistant exclusively supervises the other nine, including the next batter's readiness/helmet check while keeping the group visible. Assistant does not retrieve balls or run another activity. Waiting children may watch the parked-bat signal; no shadow swings. |
| 36–38 | **Water and conversion to running.** Secure bat, tee, and every ball. Head Coach marks a cone gate, slow-down space beyond it, and a separate return route in the now-closed ball sector. Assistant supervises water/rest, then walks the group to the start. | Running cannot start until all hitting/throwing equipment is secured and the path is clear. |
| 38–43 | **Activity 3: through first.** 38–39 demonstrate walking through the gate and slowing beyond it. Cue: “Run past first, then slow down.” 39–42:30: one child at a time, approximately 20 seconds for each short route/return; start the next only when the previous child is clear. 42:30–43: stop, count, and move into personal game spaces. Easier: walk/shorten the route. | Head Coach gives the release signal and watches the far end; Assistant keeps the other nine at waiting marks and watches return entry. No races, balls, or sliding. |
| 43–48 | **Final fun game — Baseball Statues.** 43–44 show “ready” pose, “first” point toward the gate, and “freeze” statue. 44–47 everyone responds to changing calls in their own space; children may suggest a safe funny pose. No elimination. 47–48 final shared pose, then walk to wrap-up. Easier: seated poses or pointing. | Head Coach leads; Assistant watches personal space and supports a supervised pause. All bats/balls remain secured. |
| 48–50 | **Positive close and pickup.** One optional question: “What helped you know when it was safe to go?” Recognize a real action such as waiting for a clear lane; offer an optional cheer/wave. Begin authorized handoff. | One coach retains supervision of all uncollected children while the other manages handoffs. Continue beyond 50 until all handoffs are complete. |

These are mixed-readiness tasks: provide the easier version privately and openly to anyone who wants it. For throws, move the target closer or use an underhand toss; progress by modestly increasing distance only when comfortable and controlled. For tee hitting, first check comfortable tee height/location and manageable bat; a comfortable tee adjustment is the easier retry. A broad target choice can be a later challenge. For running, walk/shorten the route before speed. Change one demand at a time. Stop for pain, fatigue, distress, unsafe behavior, or lost coverage; do not diagnose or demand a quota.

## Assistant card — print this section

> **Your assignment: one supervised waiting group, never a third station.** Ten players total; nine wait when one is active. Remain beside the waiting/water marks with everyone in view, outside bat reach and ball paths. The uncle is a spectator.
>
> **10–19 throws:** no balls with waiting players. Watch the target/ready signal. Head Coach teaches “Step toward your target.” A closer broad target or underhand toss makes the task easier; check direction and comfort after the retry. Do not add an arm correction from the sideline.
>
> **22–36 tee:** called batter only holds bat. Keep nine waiting safely; prepare the next helmet fit within your view of the group. Head Coach teaches “Send the ball into that space,” giving a clear broad purpose. Watch whether the child can swing within the space and whether the bat reaches the tee ball; an awkward setup may need a tee adjustment, not five swing fixes. Head Coach makes that change and checks the next willing attempt. Say, when true, “You parked the bat before anyone collected.”
>
> **38–43 base travel:** supervise waiting/return entry. Head Coach teaches “Run past first, then slow down.” If a child stops at the gate, re-walk the route; check whether they continue beyond before slowing.
>
> **Transitions 8–10, 19–22, 36–38:** keep children together; confirm count to Head Coach. Closed lane → gear secured → adult collection → count → move → reset → Head Coach restart. No self-directed swaps or extra practice swings/throws.
>
> **Anyone calls FREEZE.** Call Head Coach without leaving your group for a care need, child leaving the area, distress, pain/fatigue, unclear cue, poor gear fit, weather concern, or an adjustment that is not helping. Stop the active drill for lost coverage. Head Coach owns restart. Do not become a feeder, retriever, or runner while responsible for waiting children.
>
> **Before opening:** teach back waiting spots, the active route, cue, and stop/collection sequence. **After all pickups:** bring one observation and one change for next time.

## Budgeted fallbacks

- **No safe waiting zone, bad equipment fit, or sector cannot be kept empty:** preserve the clock, but replace 10–19 with ball-free target pointing and gentle step demonstrations in personal spaces, and 22–36 with bat-free ready/freeze movement and rest. Use no imaginary-bat swinging near other children. Keep the running block only if its route is safe. This retains activity without inventing a second safe sector.
- **Late Assistant or a coach must leave:** do not accept children into a live session until required approved coverage exists. If already supervising children, stop all equipment, count, keep them together, and follow the league's care/coverage procedure. Solo permission was not supplied; the uncle cannot fill in. For an assistant arriving at minute 10 with the field already safely prepared, guardians retain care through the 10–14 prebrief. Use the remaining clock: 14–17 welcome, 17–19 movement, 19–21 water/throwing setup, 21–26 throwing, 26–29 water/tee conversion, 29–39 tee, 39–41 water/running conversion, 41–44 base travel, 44–48 Statues, 48–50 wrap/handoff. Demonstrations and collection stay inside each shortened activity; expect fewer turns. If coverage or safe preparation is still unresolved, postpone safely.
- **Late player:** guardian retains care until 8–10, 19–22, or 36–38. At that stopped window, accept the handoff and demonstrate boundaries/FREEZE, trimming the next block's repetitions if needed. Do not send the child across a live sector.
- **40-minute routine version:** 0–4 welcome; 4–7 movement; 7–9 water/throwing setup; 9–15 throwing including demo and collection; 15–18 water/tee transition; 18–29 tee including demo and collection; 29–31 water/running transition; 31–35 base travel including demo; 35–38 Statues including demonstration; 38–40 wrap/handoff. Protect safe turns and record who should go first next time rather than rushing.
- **Weather or fatigue:** check actual conditions and current local policy. Use the existing activity blocks for supervised shade/rest or gentler movement when safe. Stop, shelter, reschedule, or cancel when required; the final game never overrides safety.

After every child has been handed off, spend **three adult minutes** on one success, one operational difficulty, and one agreed adjustment; then **two minutes** packing the remaining equipment. The timetable is an original planning suggestion, not a field-tested capacity claim. Local rules, exact sector dimensions, helmet fits, and live weather remain to be checked on site.
