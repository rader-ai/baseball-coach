Use the following as a **conditional 25-minute plan for tomorrow, October 12**. A shorter practice is not automatically safe in very hot weather. What city or ZIP code, practice start time, and current league heat policy apply? Those details are needed to decide whether outdoor practice should happen at all.

Before confirming practice, check the actual local conditions and the league's heat decision process, including who can cancel. If that information remains unavailable, postpone the outdoor session rather than treating this draft as clearance. If the policy permits activity but suitable shade and drinking water are unavailable, relocate to an approved cooler setting or postpone.

CDC advises limiting hot-weather outdoor activity, using cooler times of day, pacing activity, drinking water without waiting for thirst, and watching participants for heat illness. Anyone feeling weak or faint should stop and move to a cool place; seek prompt medical care for heat-illness symptoms. [CDC: Heat and Athletes](https://www.cdc.gov/heat-health/risk-factors/heat-and-athletes.html), checked October 11, 2026. This guidance does not replace local cancellation rules or establish a universal temperature cutoff.

Assume ten players, ages 7–8, two approved coaches, one soft ball, four cones, an inspected clear area, and a shaded rest area beside it. If those basic supplies are missing, use the same time blocks for shaded, equipment-free demonstrations and discussion. Water is available throughout.

Set two cones for a waiting line in shade and two for a short roll-and-return lane aimed away from that line and all other activity. Coach A leads the single active activity. Coach B supervises waiting players, water, and how everyone is feeling. Use one group rather than simultaneous stations. No bats are needed. “Freeze” means stop and hold the ball; retrieve only when all activity is stopped. Players never cross a live lane.

| Clock | Activity and adult assignment |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | In shade, both coaches count players and check how they feel. Coach A explains the stop signal and today's low-effort plan; Coach B checks water access. |
| 3–6 | Coach A leads gentle ready-position practice with no ball, in shade if the surface is suitable. Coach B watches the whole group and supports anyone resting. Cue: “Bend your knees.” Easier: seated or standing hand-position practice. |
| 6–10 | One slow ground-ball turn per child. Coach A rolls from close range; the child stops it and rolls it back, then returns to the shaded line. Coach B supervises the line and rest. Include demonstration and transitions. Cue: “Get behind it.” Easier: shorter, slower roll. Do not increase effort today. |
| 10–14 | Everyone rests and drinks in shade. Coach A asks, “Where do we look before we send a ball?” Coach B checks for discomfort. No extra drills during rest. |
| 14–18 | If conditions and the children still permit it, repeat one short roll-and-return turn each with the same adult assignments. Cue: “Look before you roll back.” Easier: Coach A retrieves the stopped ball after freezing the lane. Replace this whole block with shaded rest if needed. |
| 18–22 | Shade and water. Both coaches gather the group. Optional quiet question: “Show me ready hands.” Children may simply rest. |
| 22–25 | Brief thank-you, equipment secured, headcount, and supervised handoff. Supervision continues for any child not yet collected. |

This totals 25 minutes, including instruction, transitions, water, and closing. There are no running races, conditioning laps, or make-up repetitions.

If a coach must provide individual care or leave, stop the activity and gather everyone first. Coach B handles the affected child and guardian contact while Coach A keeps the remaining group together, following the emergency plan. Resume only with permitted, workable coverage; end early if coverage or conditions no longer support practice. If a heat emergency is suspected, activate emergency medical help rather than spending time completing the schedule.
