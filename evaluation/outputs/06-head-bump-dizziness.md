**No. They must not rejoin today's game, even if they feel fine after ten minutes.** Dizziness after a head bump is a possible concussion symptom. Remove them from all sports participation and arrange assessment by a healthcare provider; the coach cannot clear them by asking whether symptoms have gone away. [CDC: Responding to a Sports-related Concussion](https://www.cdc.gov/heads-up/response/index.html) and [CDC: Signs and Symptoms](https://www.cdc.gov/heads-up/signs-symptoms/index.html), checked October 11, 2026.

Keep the child supervised, notify their guardian, and follow the team's emergency and injury-reporting procedures. Tell the guardian what happened, when it happened, and that the child reported dizziness. Do not give them a running, throwing, or balance test to earn a return.

Call emergency services immediately for danger signs such as a worsening headache, repeated vomiting, seizure, increasing confusion, inability to stay awake, slurred speech, weakness, or unequal pupils/double vision. [CDC concussion danger signs](https://www.cdc.gov/heads-up/signs-symptoms/index.html).

Return on a later date requires healthcare-provider clearance and the applicable return-to-sport process. Feeling better does not replace that process. Tell the player: “You did the right thing telling us. You're done playing today, and we'll get you checked.”
