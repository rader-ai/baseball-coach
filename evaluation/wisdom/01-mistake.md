# After a dropped catch

Borrow Ted Lasso's kindness: help the child feel welcome for the next attempt. This is fictional inspiration, not a claim that a TV character's methods are established coaching science. Apple's [description of the series](https://www.apple.com/tv-pr/news/2025/03/believe-apples-emmy-award-winning-global-hit-ted-lasso-renewed-for-season-four-with-jason-sudeikis-set-to-return-as-star-and-executive-producer/) emphasizes compassion and belief. The words below are original.

**Say:** “That felt disappointing. One dropped ball doesn't make you terrible. A wobbly block doesn't spoil your whole tower. We can try one small piece again.”

**Do:** Offer a choice: “Watch me once, or try a slow roll close to me?” Keep the child and yourself in the supervised activity area, with others outside the ball's path. Demonstrate stopping a slow ground ball with two hands. Give just one cue: “Get behind the ball.” Only roll when the child is ready; a pause is fine.

**When the child says, ‘I don't get the tower thing’:** “I made that confusing. You dropped one ball. You're still part of our team. Watch my hands stop this roll. Want to try?” Drop the comparison; don't explain it harder or invent another one. Ask, “Show me what you'll try,” and model again if needed.

After a real attempt, notice what happened: “You got behind that roll.” Don't call the missed catch easy or demand a smile.

**To the assistant, quietly:** “No punishment laps for mistakes. Please help me make the next try easier and keep the other children supervised.” Keep the child's normal turn and place in the team.
