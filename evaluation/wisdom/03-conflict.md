# When two teammates blame each other

Borrow **Pat Summitt's emphasis on respect, responsibility, and listening**, documented by the [Pat Summitt Foundation](https://patsummitt.org/pats-legacy/). The sequence below is my adaptation for eight-year-olds, not a quotation or a claim about exactly how Summitt would handle this play.

Stop the exchange: “Pause. No blaming or name-calling. I'll listen to each of you.” If danger or physical aggression is occurring, stop play and make space immediately. For an ordinary disagreement, secure approved supervision of the rest of the group before giving the two children attention; otherwise pause the whole activity with balls held.

Give each child one short turn: “What did you see?” The other listens. They may pass. Reflect facts without guessing fault: “You both expected the other person to get the ball.” If that isn't what they described, use what they actually said.

Then choose one next action that fits the play: “On this practice roll, Alex fields it and Sam waits here.” Use those as temporary example labels, not a roster. Show each position outside the other's path, then give a slow, easy retry with a suitable practice ball. If they are still upset, offer a supervised pause; don't force an apology, handshake, or cheerful performance. Notice an actual repair: “You waited and gave your teammate room.”

**To the adult proposing to cancel the final game:** “We're keeping the game. We'll teach the behavior we need and practice it. Play isn't something the children have to win back.”

End active practice with **Baseball Statues**: secure all balls and bats; give each child clear personal space; call “ready stance,” “freeze,” or “point to first.” Everyone responds, with no elimination. One adult leads and keeps the full group in view; any second approved adult watches spacing and supports children who want help. Demonstrate each call; seated poses or pointing are easier options. Follow with one specific positive observation and supervised handoff.

If conflict remains unsafe, stop or change the activity and maintain supervision. Safety can end play; withholding a fun game to punish the team is a different decision.
