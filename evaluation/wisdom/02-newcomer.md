# A newcomer's first three minutes

Use one idea from **Dawn Staley: begin with connection.** In her [first-person essay, “The Secret,”](https://www.theplayerstribune.com/articles/dawn-staley-south-carolina-coach) she describes connection and trust as central to coaching. Offering these small choices is my beginner-baseball adaptation, not a Staley quotation or a proven intervention.

Keep this welcome in an open, visible area. An approved adult must continue supervising the rest of the group; if you are its only supervisor, include the newcomer in a whole-group demonstration instead of taking them aside.

| Time | Do and say |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:45 | Say, “Hi, I'm Coach. You can watch beside us or join this turn.” Point out a specific watching spot beside the activity, outside all ball paths, within the supervising adult's view. No required handshake, eye contact, or explanation of why they feel reluctant. |
| 0:45–1:30 | Show one slow roll and stop. If an image seems helpful: “We'll start with one little stepping stone.” Immediately make it literal: “You can watch one roll, or stop one close roll.” Keep it to that single image. |
| 1:30–2:30 | If they choose to try, use a suitable soft practice ball and a clear, short rolling lane. Show the action, then cue, “Get behind the ball.” Roll only when they signal readiness. If they choose to watch, let them watch without another invitation every few seconds. If no ball is available, offer a ball-free ready-stance demonstration instead. |
| 2:30–3:00 | If they participated, name one thing you actually saw: “You waited until the ball came to you.” Say, “Your watching spot is here; you can join the next turn when you want.” Tell the adult supervising the group which option the child chose. |

If the image confuses them, omit it and demonstrate. Joining by minute three is not a requirement; a calm, supervised place to watch is a useful first step. No speech needed.
