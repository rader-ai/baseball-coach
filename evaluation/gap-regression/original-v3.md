# 60-minute field card

**Goals:** Tee contact; control a ground ball before sending it to a ready adult target. Three mixed groups of four: A, B, C. Offer easier and harder versions to everyone, without ability labels.

**Adults/places:** You run hitting with one group; each assistant runs one fielding lane with four. Open this arrangement only after inspecting two genuinely isolated lanes inside the grass area: errant balls, waiting places and return paths cannot reach the other lane or hitting. A large area alone is not confirmation. Each adult sees their entire group. Use the tee, bat, fitted required helmets, gloves, cones/bases and soft balls; spare gear stays secured.

**FREEZE:** Stop, hold balls, park bat. No swings/throws during retrieval, crossings or changes. Only the called batter holds a bat; no child catcher. Stop for pain, fatigue, distress, unsafe conditions or lost coverage; use your established care/weather process. Water stays available.

| 58–60 | One real appreciation, optional wave, begin supervised pickup; continue until all handoffs complete. |

**Hitting:** Two swings per short turn into empty grass. “Send it into that space.” Park the bat for tee help and collection. Easier: comfortable tee adjustment. Harder: choose between broad directions within the same sector, without adding force.

**Fielding:** Adult rolls to one ready child; child stops it, then returns softly to the adult's ready glove. “Stop it, then find the target.” Easier: roll back. Harder: a gentle side roll, keeping distance unchanged. This rehearses control and direction, not a complete shortstop-to-first game play.

**If only one fielding lane fits:** Use the same clock with one group hitting, one fielding and one doing assistant-led ball-free movement. First round A/B/C respectively, then B/C/A, then C/A/B. Tell families/players that today's fielding becomes a short introduction with fewer ball attempts; don't claim the full repeated-practice goal is unchanged. If even those spaces or coverage fail, stop and reschedule.

## Preparation and opportunity decision

Fifteen adult minutes: six to inspect/mark and check equipment, five to rehearse cue/adjustment/stop and teach back each assignment, four for final conditions/care/intake readiness. Early children remain with guardians. If two fielding lanes fail the errant-ball inspection, adopt the stated smaller goal before intake. Do not count the support adult twice if a care duty removes them from a lane; stop and reassess.

Each 13-minute work block reserves 1 minute demo, 1 closing, 1 extra collection/rest and 1 interruption margin, leaving about **9 usable minutes**. In a fielding lane, a conservative **35-second full cycle** includes ready signal, roll, control, return, adult securing the ball and changing child. About 15 turns across four children means roughly 3–4 attempts each per visit, **6–8 across two visits**. Within one visit, turn starts recur about every 2 minutes 20 seconds; about 1 minute 45 seconds of that is others' turns. Extra collection increases the gap. This is a planning estimate, not a required throw count.

Trace each group: A fields 22–35 and 38–51; B fields 6–19 and 38–51; C fields 6–19 and 22–35. A/C have a three-minute water/change between visits; including end/beginning overhead and their turn position, a typical gap between last/next fielding attempts could be about 5–8 minutes. B has water, hitting and water between visits: its cross-visit gap could be about 21–24 minutes. **That is a long gap in fielding, but most of it is batting and transitions, not a 24-minute idle queue.** A lost final round or extra pause can extend these estimates; do not advertise 2:20 as whole-session spacing. Start B early in the last fielding visit if its first visit lost turns.

At hitting, allow about 60 seconds for two swings, helmet/change and reset: nine usable minutes permits about two turns each, with room for another turn for some. Starts are about four minutes apart within that one visit, plus extra collection. Each child gets the same one hitting block; rotate the starting order. All waits are visible to the assigned adult. Watching is not counted as swings.

The main plan improves fielding opportunity while preserving batting, but hitting still has a four-child queue. If the first complete cycles are slower, simplify feeds or task mechanics rather than accelerating children. Offer the experienced players direction choices and a controlled side roll; avoid turning them into unplanned feeders or making them wait to teach others.

**One-lane fallback:** A fields 22–35, B 6–19, C 38–51, about 3–4 fielding attempts each in one visit. Their other non-hitting block is supervised ball-free movement with the third adult, not ball-handling practice. There is no second fielding visit or whole-session repeat interval. This is a real reduction in the goal, not an equivalent plan; use a later session for another fielding visit. The final game remains simultaneous in either arrangement.

Late-player handoff/boundaries: use 19–22 or 35–38 while equipment is secured; keep caregiver responsibility until accepted, and take extra instruction time from the following work block. If an assistant is late, keep guardians responsible until approved coverage is established; do not open an unstaffed lane. Brief a late approved helper at a stopped break, reducing following attempts for any overrun. If safe weather merely requires an early finish after minute 38, omit the last work block, use 38–41 to secure/water, 41–45 for the simultaneous game, 45–47 to close; record the genuinely lost opportunities. Cancellation rules override this shortened plan.

After all handoffs: three minutes for teardown, three for one success, one actual wait/cycle issue, one adjustment.

## Detachable fielding assistant card — same card for either lane

**Group/time:** Four children, your assigned two blocks. One active soft ball. Stand facing the active fielder; waiting children stay to the side, outside roll/throw paths and in view. Keep the lane isolated. Receive the return yourself; no surprise throws to children.

**Show:** Slow roll → child controls → finds your ready glove → soft return. “Stop it, then find the target.” Watch control before release. If rushed, show the pause and bring the target closer; retry. Easier: rolled return. Harder: a gentle side roll with the same distance. Notice a real ready check.

**Turns:** One complete cycle, then change; plan about 35 seconds. Rotate first child. If slower, shorten/simplify rather than hurry; tell the lead coach. Waiting children may mirror a ready pose only in safe personal spaces; no independent ball activity.

**Stop:** FREEZE, retain your group, call the lead for pain/fatigue, distress, care needs, wandering or uncertainty. Retrieval only with both fielding lanes stopped. Count and move only on the lead's signal; restart after clear/readiness confirmation. Teach back before intake.
