**40-minute field card — eight beginners, two approved adults, no batting**

Today's goal: repeat a comfortable straight roll, then try moving behind a gentle roll if ready. Keep the two independent rolling lanes; leave out the long throwing route. Your current coverage, care, and pickup arrangements remain the operating plan, subject to today's conditions check.

You take four children in lane A; your assistant takes four in lane B. Each adult rolls from one end toward one ready child. Mark four waiting/ready spots beside each lane, outside its ball path and far enough apart for small ball-free movements. Check that stray balls cannot reach the other group. Use two soft balls, one per adult; park the other four at the equipment spot. Gloves, cones, and flat bases complete the setup. No child runs a separate ball station.

Say **“Freeze—hold your ball”** to stop. Everyone stops before collection, crossings, or movement between areas. The head coach restarts only when both adults have counted their groups and confirmed clear lanes. If a ball escapes, stop; nobody chases it through another activity. Water is always available under the agreed supervision arrangement.

| Clock | What to do |
|---|---|
| 0–4 | Accept handoffs, welcome the group, show boundaries and practice the stop signal. Explain: “Today we get behind gentle rolls.” |
| 4–7 | With equipment parked, adults lead their four children through ready-position copying and small side steps in personal spaces. Cue: “Get your body behind it.” Walking or pointing is fine. |
| 7–17 | **Straight roll and stop.** Demonstrate, then cycle through each group of four. Adult rolls; child stops it and rolls it back when the adult is ready; child returns to their waiting spot. Others watch where the ball goes or copy the ready shape without a ball. Start close and slow; use the same cue. Include resets and stopped collection. |
| 17–20 | Stop, park balls, count, drink, and check whether the task is comfortable. Keep groups with their adults. |
| 20–30 | **Get behind it.** Demonstrate a gentle roll just to one side. Offer straight or side rolls, changing only direction. Keep the same return and turn sequence; rotate who starts. An easier retry is a closer straight roll. Praise an observed action: “You moved behind that ball.” |
| 30–33 | Stop, secure all balls, drink, and move together to spaced personal spots for the game. |
| 33–38 | **Baseball Statues.** Call “ready,” “freeze,” or “point to first,” demonstrating each. Children copy together; invite a silly safe statue. You lead; the assistant watches spacing and the whole group. Nobody is eliminated. Easier option: seated poses or pointing. |
| 38–40 | Optional “What helped you stop the ball?”, one specific appreciation, and supervised pickup. Supervision continues until every required handoff is complete. |

Stop play if care interrupts coverage, conditions require it, or supervision becomes unworkable; follow your league arrangements before restarting. Stop a child's ball work for pain or fatigue. For an ordinary time overrun, shorten earlier rolling blocks and preserve a brief game and wrap-up; safety can end the session immediately.

**Preparation notes**

Use your ten early minutes: minutes −10 to −6 for field/conditions checks and marking the lanes, waiting spots, equipment and water area; −6 to −2 for the assistant briefing and teach-back; −2 to 0 for final readiness. Early children remain with guardians until accepted. If safe setup or approved staffing is missing, delay intake/play; do not open an unstaffed lane. Accept late arrivals at a stopped pause, including their boundary demonstration inside the remaining block.

Opportunity check: reserve two minutes of each ten-minute rolling block for demonstration, resets, and interruption margin. Eight usable minutes at an assumed 30-second complete cycle—ready, roll, stop, controlled return, and child change—allows about 16 turns per lane, or four per child per block. That is roughly eight ball attempts per child across the two blocks, enough to try a simple adjustment and retry under this model. Actual pace may be slower; observe the first round and simplify early. Waiting, warm-up, breaks, and Statues are not ball attempts. The break creates a longer gap between visits; no precise whole-session spacing is claimed. Never hurry returns to reach the estimate.

Detachable assistant card: **Four children, lane B.** Keep all four visible, with waiting children outside the ball path. Cue: “Get your body behind it.” Start with straight rolls; later offer a gentle side roll only when comfortable. Use closer/slower straight rolls for an easier retry. You control every feed and return; no independent throwing. Anyone calls stop, everyone holds, then collect only with lanes closed. Head coach coordinates restart. Teach back the places, cue, and stop sequence before intake.

After all guardian handoffs, take three adult minutes to name one success, one difficulty, and one adjustment. Allow another two minutes for teardown, longer if needed.
